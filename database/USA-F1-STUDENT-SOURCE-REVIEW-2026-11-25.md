# USA F-1 Student Visa Source Review — 25 November 2026

## Purpose and review status

This is a counsellor working reference for U.S. academic student (F-1) cases, with a distinction from M-1 vocational study. It is not legal advice and does not determine eligibility or predict a visa decision. All dashboard records remain **Pending human review** until an authorised reviewer confirms the current rules and the applicant's facts.

## 1. Correct student category and school

- F-1 generally covers academic study and language training at a qualifying SEVP-certified school.
- M-1 generally covers vocational or other recognised non-academic study.
- Verify the exact school and campus in the official DHS/SEVP School Search, and confirm the actual programme, start date, tuition and estimated living costs directly with the institution.
- An admission offer alone is not the visa document. After the school accepts the student and creates the SEVIS record, it issues Form I-20. Check the student's name, category, SEVIS ID, school, programme, start date and cost figures; student and school official signatures are required.

## 2. Application sequence and interview

1. Obtain admission from an SEVP-certified school and the correct Form I-20.
2. Check the SEVIS record and pay the I-901 SEVIS fee using the SEVIS ID on the current I-20; retain the receipt.
3. Complete Form DS-160 accurately and retain the confirmation page.
4. Follow the current U.S. Embassy/Consulate instructions for appointment scheduling, fee payment, photographs, document format and interview.
5. Prepare evidence of academic preparation, credible funding for tuition/living/travel costs, and intent to depart the United States after the authorised study period.
6. Attend interview if required. The officer may ask for additional evidence or place the case into administrative processing.

The U.S. Department of State page currently displays a USD 185 nonimmigrant visa application fee. ICE Form I-901 currently displays USD 350 for F/M students. They are separate charges; verify the live official fee/payment pages, exemptions and payment instructions immediately before quoting or paying. Never accept a payment screenshot as proof without matching the receipt to the student's current SEVIS ID.

## 3. Funding evidence

There is no universal U.S. government bank-balance figure that counsellors should quote for every F-1 applicant. Use the student's current Form I-20 and the school's documented cost estimate. Review how the student will fund tuition, living costs, travel and any relevant dependent costs. Record sponsor relationship, source of funds, availability and consistency of evidence. A bank balance by itself does not guarantee visa approval; never advise the student to borrow or temporarily park funds merely to create an artificial balance.

## 4. Visa issuance and first entry

The State Department currently states that a new F/M student visa may be issued up to 365 days before the programme start date, but a new student may not enter the United States more than 30 days before that start date. Continuing students have different rules. A visa does not guarantee admission: CBP makes the admission decision at the port of entry. Re-check the current rule, visa validity, I-20 start date and SEVIS status before travel.

## 5. Work rights — do not market as guaranteed part-time work

- F-1 on-campus employment is generally limited to 20 hours per week while school is in session, subject to status and school requirements; eligible official breaks may permit full-time work.
- Off-campus work requires a specific legal basis and the required authorisation. A student must not begin work based on a verbal promise, visa stamp alone or an unapproved internship.
- CPT must be integral to the established curriculum and directly related to the student's major. The DSO authorises it in SEVIS and endorses Form I-20 before the student begins; it cannot be backdated. One year or more of full-time CPT generally removes OPT eligibility at the same educational level.
- OPT is temporary employment directly related to the student's major. The student needs the DSO's SEVIS recommendation and an I-20 showing that recommendation before filing Form I-765. The student must wait for USCIS approval and a valid EAD before starting employment.
- A qualifying STEM graduate may have a separate 24-month STEM OPT extension, subject to the designated STEM degree/CIP code, eligible E-Verify employer, Form I-983 and filing deadlines.
- M-1 practical training is a separate regime; do not apply F-1 CPT/OPT rules to M-1 students.

### Caution about 2026 OPT timing claims

A prior source inventory note referred to a reported 2026 final rule changing post-completion OPT filing deadlines. The official source evidence retrieved for this review did not conclusively establish the final regulatory text, effective date and transition rules. Therefore, the dashboard does **not** state the reported change as settled law. Before advising on any OPT deadline, the reviewer must check the current USCIS/ICE rule text and the student's DSO/SEVIS record, including whether any transitional provision applies.

## 6. Sri Lanka applicant checks

- Follow the current U.S. Embassy/Consulate appointment instructions for the location where the applicant is applying.
- The Department of State's 15 July 2026 instruction says nonimmigrant applicants should generally schedule in their country of nationality or residence; applicants relying on residence must be able to demonstrate it. Applying elsewhere may make qualification harder.
- Check the Sri Lanka reciprocity schedule separately for visa validity/entries and any issuance fee. Do not confuse reciprocity validity with the duration of authorised stay or programme length.
- Appointment availability and processing time change; do not promise an interview date or visa turnaround.

## 7. Counsellor intake and review checklist

- [ ] Passport identity and expiry checked.
- [ ] Correct F-1 versus M-1 category confirmed with the institution.
- [ ] School and campus verified in SEVP School Search.
- [ ] Official admission and signed, current Form I-20 received.
- [ ] SEVIS ID and programme details match all records.
- [ ] I-901 fee amount/payment instructions rechecked on official ICE page; receipt matches current SEVIS ID.
- [ ] DS-160 confirmation and appointment instructions checked.
- [ ] I-20 cost estimate and credible funding evidence reviewed; no invented universal minimum.
- [ ] Academic history and course rationale are consistent and truthful.
- [ ] Applicant can explain study purpose and post-study intentions truthfully; no rehearsed or fabricated answers.
- [ ] Work-rights explanation distinguishes on-campus, CPT, OPT and M-1 practical training.
- [ ] Any OPT/CPT plan confirmed with the DSO and current USCIS/ICE rules.
- [ ] Interview location, appointment requirements and Sri Lanka reciprocity schedule checked.
- [ ] Reviewer, review date, source version and unresolved questions recorded.
