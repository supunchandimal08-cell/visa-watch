# UK Student Route — Work Rights and Employment Conditions

**Prepared:** 25 November 2026  
**Jurisdiction:** United Kingdom  
**Rule area:** Appendix Student ST 25–ST 26  
**Record status:** Needs human review before client advice  
**Primary authority:** Home Office / UK Visas and Immigration (UKVI)

## 1. Core rule

A Student's permission is subject to **no work except as specifically allowed by ST 26**. Do not tell every Student visa holder that they can work 20 hours per week.

The permitted hours depend on the sponsor type, course level and whether the study is full-time.

## 2. Main employment categories in ST 26.1

| Study / sponsor situation | During term-time | Outside term-time |
|---|---|---|
| Full-time degree-level or above course sponsored by a higher education provider with a track record of compliance | Up to 20 hours per week | Full-time employment permitted |
| Full-time below-degree-level course sponsored by a higher education provider with a track record of compliance | Up to 10 hours per week | Full-time employment permitted |
| All other study, including all part-time study | No employment permitted | Do not assume work is permitted; verify the exact route condition |

A qualifying overseas higher education institution short-term study-abroad programme in the UK is also included in the 20-hour category under ST 26.1. Confirm the exact sponsor and programme conditions.

## 3. Other important restrictions

Under ST 26 and related provisions, a Student must not assume they may:

- Be self-employed or engage in business activity, except the narrow Innovator Founder application circumstances in ST 26.8.
- Work as a professional sportsperson, including as a sports coach.
- Work as an entertainer.
- Fill a permanent full-time vacancy unless the specific Skilled Worker / Graduate route conditions in ST 26.6 are met.
- Undertake work outside the hours or conditions attached to their permission.

A qualifying work placement that is an assessed integral part of the course may be permitted under ST 26.2 and the referenced ST 17 requirements. Check the course and placement details; do not assume every internship qualifies.

## 4. Term-time and vacation checks

The sponsor's academic calendar and the student's exact course dates matter. Caseworker guidance explains that periods after course completion while Student permission remains valid are considered outside term-time for work-condition purposes. Confirm the exact course completion status and visa conditions before advising.

Do not assume public holidays, reading weeks, dissertation periods, resits, course suspension or a student's personal timetable automatically count as vacation. Confirm the official term dates and sponsor rules.

## 5. Counsellor checklist

- [ ] Exact sponsor legal entity checked against the current Student sponsor register.
- [ ] Sponsor category and track-record status confirmed.
- [ ] Course is full-time or part-time, with evidence.
- [ ] Course level is confirmed (degree level or below).
- [ ] Student's eVisa / grant conditions checked.
- [ ] Term-time and official vacation dates obtained from sponsor.
- [ ] Weekly hour limit checked against ST 26.1.
- [ ] Work placement assessed against ST 17 and ST 26.2 where relevant.
- [ ] Prohibited work and self-employment restrictions explained.
- [ ] No job, income or employment availability guarantee given.
- [ ] Named reviewer and review date recorded.

## 6. Client-safe explanation

“Some UK Student visa holders may work limited hours, but not everyone has the same permission. The limit depends on your university or sponsor, course level, study mode and term dates. Your visa conditions must be checked before you accept work. A Student visa does not guarantee a job or income.”
