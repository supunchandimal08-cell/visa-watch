# Portugal Student Residence Visa (D4) — Official Source Review — 25 November 2026

## Scope and status
This review is for non-EU/EEA/Swiss applicants resident in Sri Lanka seeking full-time higher education in Portugal (D4 residence visa → AIMA residence permit under Article 91). Secondary-school, vocational, exchange, internship, CPLP-specific and short-stay routes are not interchangeable. All dashboard rules remain **Pending human review**. Confirm the live consular checklist, programme eligibility, fees and individual exemptions before giving client advice.

## 1. Sri Lankan applicant route
- Sri Lanka has no permanent Portuguese diplomatic mission; Portugal's Ministry of Foreign Affairs country information says Sri Lanka matters are handled by the Embassy of Portugal in New Delhi. An honorary consulate exists in Colombo, but do not assume it accepts or decides visa applications.
- The competent Portuguese consular post is determined by nationality/residence. For Sri Lankan residents, contact the Embassy of Portugal in New Delhi / Consular Section and follow its current D4 instructions.
- The Embassy of Portugal in New Delhi's D4 checklist hosted by VFS Global states that documents issued in Sri Lanka (including academic certificates and civil/criminal records) must be authenticated by Sri Lanka's Ministry of Foreign Affairs and then legalised by the relevant Embassy/High Commission in New Delhi. Confirm the exact sequence and current requirements with the consular section before legalising originals.
- Do not direct Sri Lankan residents to VFS Colombo for a Portuguese D4 residence visa based on short-stay Schengen representation. Portugal's short-stay visa representation and long-stay national D4 competence are different questions.
- Do not assume Sri Lankan residents may lodge through VFS India; the current residence-based eligibility for the India VFS centre must be confirmed. Obtain written confirmation from the New Delhi Consular Section on the accepted filing method, appointment route and document delivery.

## 2. Entry route and September 2026 legal change
- Higher-education students use the national residence visa for study (commonly called D4), then apply to AIMA for the higher-education residence permit under Article 91.
- A residence visa is not itself the residence permit. After entry, the student must complete the AIMA residence-permit process and meet enrolment, tuition (if applicable), funds, accommodation/address, criminal-record and health-cover requirements.
- Lei n.º 62/2026, published 10 September 2026 and effective 11 September 2026, revoked Article 91(4), which had allowed a higher-education student who entered Portugal legally to seek the Article 91 permit without first holding the study residence visa. Do not advise a student to enter as a visitor and convert in-country under the former Article 91(4) route.
- The law also revoked other in-country exceptions in Articles 92(4) and 93(3). Do not generalise the higher-education route to secondary, vocational or internship categories.

## 3. Admission and documents
- Applicant must have been admitted or meet admission conditions for a recognised Portuguese higher-education institution/programme.
- D4 residence visa checklist commonly includes: national visa application form; valid passport; photographs; proof of admission/acceptance; proof of tuition payment where applicable; proof of means; accommodation evidence; travel/health insurance; criminal record certificate from country of nationality or country where resident for over one year; consent for Portuguese criminal-record consultation; return/onward travel evidence where requested; and a personal cover letter.
- Public documents must be authenticated/legalised in the correct chain and translated where required by the competent consular post. For Sri Lankan-issued documents, the New Delhi D4 checklist specifically refers to Sri Lanka MOFA authentication followed by legalisation at the relevant Embassy/High Commission in New Delhi.
- Confirm the exact list and any exemptions on the current D4 checklist. Do not use a tourist-visa checklist.

## 4. 2026 means of subsistence
- Portaria n.º 1563/2007, Article 5(4), says a residence-visa applicant for study must have means of subsistence for 12 months (or the number of months of stay in an exchange programme). The base calculation refers to the minimum monthly wage.
- Portugal's Decreto-Lei n.º 139/2025 fixes the 2026 national minimum wage (RMMG) at €920/month from 1 January 2026. For one adult, the arithmetic benchmark is €920 × 12 = €11,040 for 12 months.
- Where accommodation is proven to be provided, the amount may be reduced by half. Where both accommodation and food are proven to be provided, the amount may be reduced by up to 90%, under Article 5(4) of Portaria 1563/2007. These are reductions subject to acceptable evidence and consular assessment, not automatic discounts.
- The general family calculation in Article 2 is first adult 100%, each additional adult 50%, each child under 18 / dependent child 30%; assess the actual applicant/family composition and period.
- AIMA's Article 91 page specifically says Camões Institute scholarship beneficiaries are exempt from submitting evidence of means (and certain other documents). Do not assume every university scholarship, university, or student is exempt without written official confirmation of the applicable exemption.
- Do not present €11,040 as a universally fixed published student balance; it is the 2026 12-month calculation from the official minimum-wage benchmark. Confirm the current consular evidence format and any permitted sponsor undertaking.

## 5. Tuition and health cover
- At AIMA Article 91 residence-permit stage, the student must provide proof of enrolment and proof of payment of tuition fees if applicable.
- AIMA requires evidence that the student is covered by the Portuguese National Health Service or has health insurance.
- Tuition amounts are institution/course-specific; quote only from the current university offer/invoice. Distinguish application fee, tuition deposit, annual tuition and living funds.

## 6. Work while studying
- Article 97(1) of Lei n.º 23/2007 states that holders of a residence permit granted under the study sub-section may exercise subordinate (employee) or independent (self-employed) professional activity in addition to the activity that gave rise to the visa.
- Article 97(2) and (3) are marked revoked in the current consolidated law. The current text does not state a general 20-hour weekly cap for Article 91 higher-education residence-permit holders.
- Work rights arise from the granted residence permit, not merely from an admission letter or a pending D4 visa. Do not promise a job, income or that work can cover the living-funds requirement.
- This rule must not be generalised to secondary-school students, unpaid trainees, volunteers or other permit categories.

## 7. Permit duration and renewal
- AIMA states that the Article 91 higher-education residence permit is valid for three years and renewable for equal periods; if the study programme is shorter than three years, it is issued for the programme duration.
- EU/multilateral mobility programmes and institutional agreements have separate validity provisions (normally two years or programme duration, subject to statutory conditions).
- Keep enrolment, academic progress, tuition status, address, insurance and sufficient means evidence ready for renewal. Check current AIMA appointment/online platform availability; the AIMA page says online platform implementation for residence-visa holders is in progress.

## 8. Post-study option — limited scope
- AIMA Article 122(1)(p) route provides up to one year for eligible former holders of a higher-education residence permit for second- or third-cycle study (master's / postgraduate specialisation or doctorate) who completed studies, to seek work or create a company compatible with their qualifications.
- AIMA's stated evidence includes valid passport, address declaration, means of subsistence evidenced by an employment contract/promise, service contract or declaration of commencement of independent activity, authenticated criminal record and proof of completion / intention to seek qualifying work.
- Do not promise this one-year route to every bachelor's graduate. The AIMA text specifically names second-/third-cycle graduates and research permit holders; any other graduate pathway requires separate current legal confirmation.
- This is a job-search/business-creation period, not a job placement or automatic permanent residence.

## 9. Family
- Family reunification is a separate procedure under the current law and depends on the student's permit, qualifying family relationship, timing, accommodation and subsistence requirements. Do not promise spouse/child approval or assume they receive the student's student work conditions. Confirm current AIMA family-reunification rules and transitional provisions for the applicant's case.

It also notes that the digital filing platform for residence-visa holders is still being implemented; do not promise a fully online filing route.

Keep the two sources distinct: use the 2026 statutory decree for the €920 calculation and label the AIMA means page as framework guidance with stale annual wage text until AIMA refreshes it.
- Reopened Portaria n.º 1563/2007. Article 5(4) explicitly sets 12 months (or exchange-programme duration) and allows a reduction of half when accommodation is provided, or up to 90% when both accommodation and food are provided. These reductions require evidence and must not be treated as automatic.
- Reopened the consolidated Lei n.º 23/2007: Article 91(1) requires enrolment, tuition payment if applicable, means and health cover; Article 91(2) sets three-year validity (or programme duration if shorter). Article 97 authorises employee or self-employed activity for holders of the relevant study residence permit. The current text does not set a general weekly-hour cap in Article 97(1).
- Reopened Lei n.º 62/2026 and confirmed its amendment is effective from 11 September 2026. Article 91(4) was revoked; do not advise higher-education applicants to rely on the former in-country no-residence-visa conversion route.

- AIMA's Article 122(1)(p) post-study route remains limited to eligible second-/third-cycle graduates and research-permit holders, up to one year; do not market it as a universal graduate work visa.
- Sri Lankan consular jurisdiction and VFS India eligibility remain operational checks: confirm directly with the Embassy of Portugal in New Delhi before booking, paying or legalising original documents.
