# Europe / Schengen Student Visa — Jurisdiction Review — 25 November 2026

## Key counsellor rule: do not describe Europe as one student visa destination

There is no single “Schengen student visa” that gives a non-EU student a right to reside and study long-term in every Schengen country. A short-stay Schengen visa is not a substitute for the destination country's national long-stay student visa and/or residence permit.

The European Commission explains that the EU and Member States share immigration competence, but rules are not identical in every country. National authorities decide individual applications and issue residence permits. Denmark does not apply EU-wide immigration, visa and asylum rules; Ireland decides case by case whether to opt into relevant EU measures.

Schengen membership and EU membership are not the same thing. Do not use these labels interchangeably in client explanations.

## Mandatory intake questions before advising

1. Which exact country will the student live and study in?
2. What is the course level, exact duration, institution, campus and start date?
3. Is the student applying from Sri Lanka, and which embassy/visa centre has jurisdiction?
4. Is the institution officially recognised/authorised for international students under that country's rules?
5. What are the national student visa/residence permit requirements for admission, tuition, living funds, insurance, language, biometrics and police/medical evidence?
6. What work rights apply to that exact student permit, and are there weekly/hourly limits or employer permits?
7. What post-study route exists for that country and qualification, and what are its conditions?
8. Are there any country-specific deadlines, appointment restrictions or transitional rules?

## Germany — dated 2026 fact check

The German federal government portal currently states:
- Student living funds for 2026: at least €992 net per month / €11,904 for 12 months.
- Evidence can include a blocked account, scholarship or Declaration of Commitment.
- Third-country students may work up to 140 full days or 280 half-days per calendar year; alternatively, up to 20 hours per week during lecture periods. Student assistant activities are treated differently.
- A qualifying German graduate may generally obtain up to 18 months to seek qualified employment after graduation.

These are national-level guidance points. The applicant's exact programme, permit wording, funds, tuition, embassy instructions and current statutory position must be checked before advice.

## Do not make these claims to clients

- “One Schengen student visa lets you study anywhere in Europe.”
- “All European countries have the same bank balance requirement.”
- “All European student visas allow the same part-time work hours.”
- “A Schengen tourist visa can be converted automatically into a student residence permit.”
- “A student is guaranteed a job or post-study work visa after graduation.”
