# Slovakia — Student Immigration Source Review
**Review date:** 25 November 2026  
**Status:** Research draft — all dashboard rules remain **Pending human review**.  
**Scope:** Non-EU/EEA applicants, including Sri Lankan nationals; higher education and qualifying secondary/language preparation.

## 1. Main route
A third-country national admitted to an eligible Slovak university generally applies for temporary residence for the purpose of study under Section 24 of Act No. 404/2011. The permit is tied to the study purpose and may be granted for the expected duration of studies, up to a maximum of six years. The student must meet the statutory eligibility and document requirements. University study is the principal category; secondary-school and language/preparatory routes have separate age/course conditions and must not be conflated with university study.

The Slovak authorities also operate a national D visa route for study/research. The current Embassy of Slovakia in New Delhi study page describes a university-study D visa application and appointment capacity through the institution's RegVIS registration. Applicants must follow the mission's exact route: national D visa issuance and temporary residence application are distinct legal steps and not interchangeable with a Schengen short-stay C visa.

## 2. Sri Lankan consular route — verify before advising
The Embassy of Slovakia in New Delhi's official residence guidance specifically says that documents from Sri Lanka must be legalised by the relevant Ministry of Foreign Affairs and then superlegalised by the Embassy of Slovakia in New Delhi before further copying/translation steps. The Embassy's national D study page provides the study document list and RegVIS process. Separately, the Slovak MFA's Schengen representation page lists Italy as representing Slovakia for short-stay Schengen visa applications in Colombo. That short-stay representation does NOT establish that Italy accepts Slovak national D study visas or temporary residence applications.

Accordingly, counsellors must not tell a Sri Lankan applicant that the Slovak D visa is lodged in Colombo or through the Italian Embassy. Confirm directly with the Slovak Embassy in New Delhi whether it accepts the applicant's national D study application, the current appointment/RegVIS workflow, legalisation sequence, fees and submission arrangements. Verify if any current Slovak diplomatic/consular post has changed the territorial competence.

## 3. University study D visa — checklist
The current New Delhi Embassy page for university study lists: valid passport (at least 90 days beyond intended departure), national visa application form, temporary residence application form, recent 3 × 3.5 cm photograph, EUR 90 administrative fee, Slovak-language decision confirming admission to university studies, criminal-record extract(s) with apostille/superlegalisation and official Slovak translation, flight reservation, and health insurance covering medical expenses in Slovakia from intended entry until expiry of the national visa (the page specifies 120 days). Conditional admission may require recognition of the foreign secondary-school leaving certificate. The institution arranges study/research appointment capacity through RegVIS. Applicants submitting IELTS or TOEFL may not require a personal interview, according to the Embassy page; confirm the current practice.

The Embassy page's university D checklist does not show a standalone bank-funds item in the same list. Do not import the EUR 56/day language-school figure into a university student's D visa checklist. Temporary-residence financial means are a separate requirement and should be checked against the latest residence rules.

## 4. Temporary residence — documents, duration and financial means
The Slovak MFA residence guidance (updated 15 August 2026) lists a valid travel document, admission confirmation from the competent authority or educational institution, and criminal-record extract(s) from the applicant's country of nationality and any country where the applicant stayed more than 90 days during six consecutive months in the last three years. Documents generally must not be older than 90 days. Accommodation proof is required for secondary-school students; the MFA's current list does not require it for university students under the study category. The permit can cover the expected study duration, up to six years.

The general residence financial rule requires funds equal to the Slovak subsistence minimum for each month of residence; if the intended residence exceeds one year, the applicant demonstrates 12 times the subsistence minimum. The Ministry of Labour's official amount effective 1 July 2026 is EUR 295.22 per month for one adult; 12 months therefore equals EUR 3,542.64. This is a statutory residence financial test, not a realistic total living-cost budget and not tuition. Verify the accepted evidence form and the calculation against the latest competent mission/Foreign Police instructions. The MFA page accepts, among other items, an account-balance confirmation in the applicant's own name; scholarship confirmation; a legal entity's undertaking; or a sponsor's affidavit accompanied by the sponsor's last three months of bank statements. Some documents must be no older than 90 days.

## 5. Work during study — university vs other students
The Slovak MFA's current foreign-student page states that a university student holding temporary residence for study may work without an employment permit for up to 80 hours per month. The EU Immigration Portal expresses this as up to 20 hours per week for university students and 10 hours per week for other students. The Slovak study portal likewise states 80 hours/month for university students and 40 hours/month for high-school students. Do not promise unrestricted full-time work: work above the relevant limit requires the appropriate change of residence purpose/employment route. Confirm the exact current category and calculation with the Foreign Police before client advice.

## 6. After graduation — 9-month job-search extension
Official Slovak study and research portals state that a graduate who successfully completes full university studies in Slovakia may apply to extend temporary residence for nine months to seek employment or start a business. Evidence includes a Slovak university diploma or confirmation of completion and proof of financial coverage. A separate 30-day protective period after successful graduation is also described in some official/European guidance; do not treat that 30-day period as a substitute for timely application for the nine-month extension. The extension is not a job offer, employment guarantee or automatic permanent residence.

## 7. Decision time and application management
The Slovak MFA foreign-student page states that the Foreign Police decides on a complete study-residence application within 30 days. This should not be treated as a guaranteed end-to-end timeline: appointment access, RegVIS, document legalisation/translation, missing documents and individual checks can add time. Confirm the current statutory deadline and local Foreign Police procedure at filing.

## Counsellor checklist
- Verify the institution and programme, full-time university status, study duration, admission decision and any secondary-school certificate recognition.
- Confirm whether the applicant needs national D visa and/or temporary residence filing, and follow the Slovak Embassy New Delhi's current RegVIS and jurisdiction instructions.
- For Sri Lankan documents, verify legalisation by the relevant Sri Lankan Ministry of Foreign Affairs and superlegalisation by the Slovak Embassy in New Delhi; confirm current process and document order.
- Do not confuse Italy's short-stay Schengen representation in Colombo with Slovak national D visa acceptance.
- Check criminal records, official Slovak translations, passport validity, insurance dates and exact document freshness.
- Calculate residence funds using the subsistence minimum effective on the filing date; 1 July 2026 amount for one adult is EUR 295.22/month. Tuition and actual living costs are separate.
- Explain the university work limit (80 hours/month / 20 hours per week) and distinguish other student categories.
- Explain the nine-month graduate job-search/business route and submit before the existing status expires.
- Record source URL, access date, publication/effective date, reviewer and decision. Keep all rules Pending human review until approved.

## 8. Targeted official-source recheck — 26 November 2026

- Reopened the Slovak MFA residence guidance (updated 16 August 2026). It confirms Section 24 study temporary residence, a maximum six-year duration, official Slovak translations for foreign documents, criminal-record extracts for nationality and qualifying recent residence countries, and the general financial-means rule. For university students, the current study-specific checklist does not require accommodation evidence; the general accommodation rule should not be mechanically copied into the university-student checklist. Secondary-school students have separate accommodation and age/course requirements.
- Rechecked the Ministry of Labour official subsistence-minimum table effective 1 July 2026: EUR 295.22/month for one adult. The general residence means formula is the amount for each month of stay, capped at 12 months in advance for stays over one year; the one-adult 12-month figure is EUR 3,542.64. This is a statutory residence-means test, not a complete cost-of-living estimate or tuition amount.
- Reopened the Embassy of Slovakia in New Delhi current National D visa for Study page. For university study, the checklist specifies EUR 90, passport valid at least 90 days beyond intended departure, the national visa and temporary-residence forms, Slovak-language admission decision, criminal-record extract(s) with legalisation and Slovak translation, flight reservation and health insurance from entry until visa expiry (the page specifies 120 days). The university checklist does not list the EUR 56/day amount; that figure is expressly for language-school applications. Do not transfer it to university applicants.
- New operational warning from the Embassy appointment notice updated 10 August 2026 for academic year 2025/2026: the admitted institution must submit the appointment request; students and agents cannot request it directly. After submission at BLS/VFS, the applicant must attend a mandatory personal interview at the Embassy the following day. The 2025/2026 notice is time-bound; the 2026/2027 schedule must be confirmed before lodging. RegVIS/institution registration does not guarantee a specific appointment.
- New Delhi Embassy residence instructions explicitly say Sri Lankan documents must be legalised by the relevant Sri Lankan Ministry of Foreign Affairs and then superlegalised by the Slovak Embassy in New Delhi before other steps. The same page lists Italy's Colombo role only for short-stay Schengen visa representation; it is not proof that Italy accepts Slovak national D visas or residence applications.
- Slovak MFA student guidance says university students with study-purpose temporary residence may work up to 80 hours per month without an employment permit; work beyond that requires the relevant employment-purpose route. The European Commission describes the university limit as 20 hours per week and other student categories as 10 hours per week. Keep category distinctions and do not promise employment or income.
- Official guidance continues to state a nine-month extension for eligible university graduates seeking work or starting a business, subject to proof of completion and financial coverage. This is not a job guarantee.
