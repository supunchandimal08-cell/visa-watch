# Belgium Student Visa — Official Source Review (25 November 2026)

## Scope and status
This review covers non-EEA applicants seeking full-time higher education in Belgium, including applicants applying from Sri Lanka. Belgium has federal immigration rules and regional/community education and employment administration. The exact institution, programme, region, residence-card wording and applicant nationality matter. This is a counsellor reference, not legal advice. Dashboard entries remain **Pending human review**.

## 1. Correct route: long-stay visa D
- Most non-EEA nationals who intend to study in Belgium for more than 90 days need a long-stay visa D before travel. A short-stay Schengen C visa is not the correct route for a normal long-term degree programme.
- **Sri Lanka-specific application channel:** the Belgian Embassy's page for people living in Sri Lanka states Belgium has no visa section in Sri Lanka. Switzerland's representation in Colombo applies only to specified short-stay C visas and does not cover long-stay student visas. Long-stay D applications, including student visas, must be lodged at VFS New Delhi. For procedural clarification, the Embassy lists newdelhi.visa@diplobel.fed.be.
- The Embassy's Sri Lanka page (updated 30 June 2026) lists a **€250 handling fee** for a long-stay D visa. Certain D applications also require a separate additional contribution paid according to Immigration Office instructions. These are distinct charges; verify the live amount, payment method and whether the additional contribution applies before quoting.
- Study in Flanders indicates that normal processing can take six to eight weeks; this is not a guaranteed decision time. Apply early and do not book non-refundable travel based on an assumed date.
- For the standard higher-education route under the Belgian Immigration Act, the institution/programme and the legal basis must be checked. A private institution or non-recognised programme may follow a different route (for example, Article 9 rather than the standard Article 58 higher-education route). Do not assume that admission to any private college automatically qualifies for the standard student residence status.

## 2. Financial resources — academic year 2026–2027
The Belgian Immigration Office (Office des Étrangers / Dienst Vreemdelingenzaken) confirms the minimum amount is **€1,062 net per month** for 2026–2027. It is indexed annually.
For a 12-month blocked-account calculation, the base amount is €12,744 (12 × €1,062), before any institution/provider administration charges.
The Immigration Office lists accepted evidence routes including:
- Deposit of 12 times the monthly amount into a blocked account opened by the higher-education institution in the student's name;
- A qualifying blocked account via a specialised financial-services provider. The Immigration Office page currently names accepted irrevocable-transfer certificates from Studely and Ready Study Go International; verify the live list before recommending a provider;
- Scholarship/loan certificate from a qualifying public body or higher-education institution;
- Annex 32 formal financial undertaking by an eligible guarantor.
The assessment is individual. A generic family sponsorship letter or a normal bank balance should not be assumed sufficient. Tuition, travel, insurance, accommodation deposits and other setup costs are separate from the monthly living-funds requirement.

## 3. Work during studies
For a non-EEA student with valid student residence status and the appropriate “labour market: limited” notation on the electronic A-card:
- Up to **20 hours per week** during the academic year, provided work does not interfere with studies;
- The 20-hour restriction does not apply during official school holidays.
Check the current residence card, study status, regional employment rules and applicable student employment conditions. Do not promise a job, guaranteed hours or earnings. Work should not be the funding basis for visa eligibility.

## 4. Arrival registration and residence card
After arrival, the student must register with the municipality/city hall where they actually reside and follow its identity/address verification procedure. The precise appointment deadline and documents should be checked with the municipality and competent Belgian authority. Bring the passport and visa, proof of enrolment, accommodation/address evidence and health-insurance evidence as instructed. Do not apply one municipality's local checklist to every Belgian city.

## 5. Renewal and study progress
Apply for renewal before the A-card expires, following the municipality's and Immigration Office's current deadlines. Renewal depends on continuing to meet the student-residence conditions, including genuine study progress and sufficient resources. Check the applicable academic-progress rules for the student's programme and year; do not quote a universal credit threshold without confirming the current legal provision and any permitted exceptions.

## 6. After graduation — search year
A qualifying graduate may request authorisation to extend their stay for up to **12 months** to look for work or establish a business. This is a separate application, not an automatic extension and not a job guarantee. Confirm eligibility, application timing, financial/insurance conditions and the route to a subsequent work or self-employment residence permit with the Immigration Office.

## Counsellor checklist
1. Confirm nationality, legal residence, course level, exact institution, campus, programme duration and region (Flanders, Wallonia, Brussels or German-speaking Community).
2. Verify whether the institution/programme qualifies for the standard higher-education student residence route; distinguish public/recognised higher education from private/non-standard institutions.
3. For Sri Lanka residents, direct long-stay D student applicants to the VFS New Delhi channel specified by the Belgian Embassy. Do not confuse this with Switzerland's short-stay C-visa representation in Colombo.
4. Confirm the €250 D-visa handling fee and whether a separate additional contribution applies; recheck payment instructions before filing.
5. Calculate 12 months of funds at €1,062/month for 2026–2027; keep tuition and other expenses separate. Confirm accepted proof route with the Immigration Office and institution.
6. Explain the 20-hour term-time work rule and school-holiday exception; clarify that work is not guaranteed and must not interfere with studies.
7. Explain municipal registration and A-card formalities after arrival.
8. Explain renewal and post-study search-year eligibility as conditional, not automatic.
9. Record official source, check date, reviewer and approval. Keep all rules Pending human review until authorised approval.
