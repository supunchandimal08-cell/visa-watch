# Poland student-route source review — 26 November 2026

**Status: Research record — Pending human review. Not legal advice or approval.**
**Applicant context used for routing check:** Sri Lankan non-EU applicant considering full-time higher education in Poland.
**Scope:** National D visa for study, study-purpose temporary residence permit, funds, work, post-study route and Sri Lanka consular jurisdiction.

## 1. Correct route distinction

- A stay longer than 90 days for study generally requires the Polish national long-stay D visa and/or a Polish temporary residence permit for study, depending on the applicant's location, existing status and intended duration.
- A short-stay Schengen C visa is not a substitute for a national long-stay student route.
- The EU Immigration Portal page dated 16 September 2025 is useful background but contains financial figures that differ from current Polish Office for Foreigners / MOS guidance. Use the competent Polish authority's current procedure and recheck before filing.
- Eligibility for a study-purpose temporary residence permit is not universal for every course. The official MOS definition includes full-time first- and second-cycle studies, long-cycle master's studies, doctoral school, and specified preparatory courses at eligible institutions. Part-time, postgraduate and MBA education may fall under a different residence category.

## 2. Application process and 2026 electronic filing change

The government-supported study.gov.pl visa guidance states that from **27 April 2026**, applications for study-purpose temporary residence permits must be submitted electronically through the MOS portal. The application remains handled by the competent Voivode; electronic filing does not remove subsequent in-person requirements such as fingerprints, original travel document presentation or additional evidence. The university must complete and electronically sign its annex before the student can submit. The applicant must electronically sign and retain the official acknowledgement of receipt (UPO).

This is a temporary-residence-permit procedure change, not a statement that the initial D-type national visa is filed through MOS. Initial D visa applications are a separate consular process.

Official source:
- Study.gov.pl — visa application / temporary residence permit: https://study.gov.pl/visa-application

## 3. Funds — important current-source conflict

The current MOS study-and-science Q&A and MOS study permit page state a monthly living-cost threshold of:
- **PLN 1,010/month** for a single applicant without dependants;
- **PLN 823/month** for the applicant and each dependant supported, subject to the formula and category described by MOS.

The MOS calculation requires actual housing costs to be accounted for separately; documented fixed accommodation charges and utilities are relevant. Return funds are also required: PLN 2,500 for a destination outside the neighbouring-country / specified EU categories, which is the relevant band for a Sri Lankan applicant under the published MOS table.

The official government-supported D visa checklist for the Embassy of Poland in India, dated 14 May 2026, separately states PLN 1,010/month, accommodation costs and PLN 2,500 return funds for a study visa. Tuition / study-fee payment evidence is also part of the visa checklist where applicable.

The older European Commission EU Immigration Portal page (16 September 2025) states PLN 776 for a single person / PLN 600 for a person in a family. These older figures conflict with current MOS material and the 2026 consular checklist. Do not use the older figures as the current planning amount without a fresh written confirmation from the competent authority.

Do not reduce required proof of funds by assumed part-time earnings. Do not treat the amount as a universal all-in budget: tuition, accommodation, insurance, return travel and other required costs must be assessed separately.

## 4. Work during studies

The Polish government-supported study.gov.pl work guidance states that a non-EU/EEA citizen enrolled in **full-time studies** in Poland and staying on a Polish national visa issued for study purposes or a temporary residence permit for studies may work without a separate work permit. Different rules can apply to part-time study or a different residence basis.

This is not a guarantee of a job, unlimited practical availability, or permission to disregard study obligations. The official guidance says studies must remain the main purpose of stay. Confirm the exact permit annotation, enrolment status and current legal basis for the individual case. Expected earnings must not be counted as visa proof of funds.

Official source:
- Study.gov.pl — work and internships: https://study.gov.pl/work-and-internships

## 5. After graduation

The same government-supported study.gov.pl guidance states that graduates of full-time studies at a Polish higher education institution may work without a work permit. That work exemption does not itself extend lawful stay. A non-EU graduate must maintain a valid legal basis to remain in Poland.

A graduate who wants to stay to look for work or start a business may be eligible for a one-time temporary residence permit for graduates for **nine months**, subject to the current legal requirements and timely application. This is not automatic and does not guarantee employment.

Official source:
- Study.gov.pl — work and internships: https://study.gov.pl/work-and-internships

## 6. Sri Lanka consular jurisdiction — D visa

The official Embassy of Poland in India D-type study visa page states that the Embassy in New Delhi accepts applications from Sri Lankan citizens (or residents) within its stated jurisdiction. It says study visa applications are registered through e-Konsulat and submitted personally at a Visa Application Centre, with a separate mandatory VFS form for study applicants. The page currently identifies New Delhi as the responsible mission for Sri Lanka; confirm the live appointment and VAC instructions before any travel or payment.

The Embassy's official consular-jurisdiction page also lists Sri Lanka within the New Delhi Consular Section's territory. Do not confuse this D-visa jurisdiction with the separate C-type Schengen representation arrangement.

## 7. Insurance, institution and course checks

- Medical insurance is required; the exact acceptable policy, coverage, dates and insurer conditions must be checked against the current Polish consular checklist and current official insurer list. Do not assume any travel policy qualifies.
- Verify the exact institution and course's eligibility for the intended residence category. The MOS guidance distinguishes full-time studies from other forms of education such as part-time, postgraduate and MBA study.
- Confirm tuition payment / fee evidence, admission/enrolment documentation, accommodation, return funds and all consular checklist items for the applicant's own case.
- The Embassy of Poland in India D-study page currently references a 14 May 2026 checklist. Always download the latest checklist at the time of application.

## 8. Counsellor-safe summary

1. First confirm the institution, course level, full-time status and whether it qualifies as statutory “studies”.
2. For an applicant in Sri Lanka, use the official Poland-in-India D-study instructions and verify the current New Delhi / VAC process before booking or paying.
3. Keep the D-visa process separate from the MOS electronic temporary-residence application process introduced from 27 April 2026.
4. For current planning, note the MOS PLN 1,010 single-person monthly figure and PLN 2,500 return-cost band, plus separately evidenced housing, tuition and insurance. Explicitly disclose the older EU Portal discrepancy; obtain written confirmation if the amount or category materially affects the case.
5. Explain the full-time study work-permit exemption carefully; it is not a job promise and must not be included in proof-of-funds.
6. Explain the possible nine-month graduate permit as conditional and one-time, not automatic.
7. Record source publication/update dates, exact URL, applicant course/permit category and the reviewer decision.
