# Croatia — student residence source review
**Review date:** 25 November 2026  
**Jurisdiction:** Republic of Croatia (Schengen Member State)  
**Applicant lens:** non-EU/EEA/Swiss applicant resident in Sri Lanka  
**Record status:** Pending human review — not legal advice

## 1. Correct route and scope
Croatia does not have a single Schengen student visa. For university studies, the primary immigration permission is **temporary stay for the purpose of university studies** under Croatian national law. A Sri Lankan ordinary-passport holder requires a visa to enter Croatia. Where the student first obtains temporary-stay approval, the separate **long-stay visa D** is the entry document used by a visa-required third-country national to enter Croatia and complete residence formalities. Do not describe a Schengen short-stay C visa as the normal degree-student route.

The Croatian Ministry of the Interior (MUP) says a visa-required third-country national admitted to undergraduate, graduate or postgraduate university studies may exceptionally submit the temporary-stay application at the competent police administration/station while legally present in Croatia. This is a narrow statutory exception; it is not permission to enter as a visitor and assume approval or conversion. Confirm the applicant's exact entry status and application method with MUP/consular staff before travel.

## 2. Eligibility and documents
MUP's study-specific guidance requires study at a Croatian higher-education institution, sufficient means for subsistence and return travel, and proof of enrolment. General temporary-stay conditions include:
- proof of the purpose of stay / university enrolment;
- valid travel document;
- evidence of subsistence funds and return-travel funds;
- health insurance;
- first-application criminal-record certificate, subject to the specific statutory exceptions (the published student exception is limited to students arriving from another EEA Member State; do not assume a Sri Lankan applicant is exempt);
- no entry/stay ban or relevant SIS alert and no public-policy, national-security or public-health threat.

The EU Immigration Portal's Croatia page lists passport copy, 30 x 35 mm photograph, financial evidence, health insurance, duly legalised criminal-record certificate, proof of higher-education enrolment and parental authorisation for minors. Confirm the current Croatian checklist, translation and legalisation requirements before collecting originals.

## 3. 2026 financial calculation
MUP's current third-country-national funds page cites the Croatian average monthly net salary for 2025 as **€1,449**, published 4 March 2026. For a temporary stay for the purpose of studying, the student may provide:
- scholarship evidence; or
- monthly subsistence funds of at least **25%** of that average net salary = **€362.25 per month**; and
- separate return-travel funds of at least **60%** of the same average net salary = **€869.40**.

These are calculations from the MUP-published percentages and 2025 salary base, not tuition or an all-in living budget. The applicant must also budget tuition, accommodation, insurance, travel and other real expenses. Confirm what account statements/scholarship evidence are accepted under the current rule and whether the salary base is updated during the application period.

## 4. Permit duration, renewal and registration
MUP says temporary stay is granted for up to one year. The EU Immigration Portal says the student permit may be issued for one year or the shorter study period; mobility-programme cases may receive up to two years. The residence card is biometric and issued in Croatia. The EU portal says an extension application should be filed no later than 60 days before expiry; verify the live MUP procedure and any newer rule before advising.

Address-registration wording differs between the general MUP page and the study-specific FAQ/EU portal. MUP's study FAQ states address registration within 15 days after entry and, where approval was granted through a diplomatic mission, further reporting within 30 days after approval (or six months from D-visa validity start where a D visa is required); the general MUP page says address reporting within three days. **Do not use one deadline generically**: obtain the applicant's exact entry/approval route and confirm the applicable deadline with the competent police station. The study FAQ also says the student should contact the Croatian Health Insurance Fund within eight days after the approval decision becomes enforceable to determine health-insurance rights.

## 5. Work during studies
The Aliens Act amendment NN 55/2026, Article 16, amended Article 89 to allow university-study temporary-stay holders to work as employees, self-employed or through student-work arrangements for **no more than 25 hours per week**, unless the student internship is an integral part of the study programme. The amendment was published on 27 May 2026 and Article 70 provides it enters into force on the eighth day after publication: **4 June 2026**. The MUP public work webpage still displays the old 20-hour wording; this is a stale administrative page and must not override the later enacted law. This is a statutory ceiling, not a promise of a job or income. Confirm the student has the correct university-study temporary-stay status and check the applicable student-work arrangements before advising.

## 6. Tuition and admission
Croatian higher-education institutions set their own tuition. The national Study in Croatia portal publishes broad indicative annual ranges for international students: humanities/social sciences about €1,100–€10,400; natural/technical sciences about €1,000–€6,000; medical studies about €10,000–€12,000. It also notes possible application fees of €60–€300. These are broad indicative ranges, not a university quote. Confirm exact programme language, entry qualification recognition, tuition, deposit/refund terms and enrolment confirmation directly with the institution.

## 7. D visa and Sri Lanka filing route
Croatian MFA says a D visa is issued to a visa-required third-country national after temporary stay has been granted (or in the specific statutory case where the applicant is applying for temporary stay at a police station). It is normally valid for up to six months with an approved stay of up to 30 days, and the holder must contact the competent police station within 30 days from the visa's validity start to register residence and obtain the biometric permit. The D visa is not a one-year residence permit.

Croatian MFA identifies the Embassy of Croatia in New Delhi as the diplomatic mission covering Sri Lanka; an Honorary Consulate is also listed in Colombo. The MFA country page lists a visa application centre in Colombo, but a separate New Delhi mission instruction page says Sri Lankan applicants may be required to appear at the Embassy in New Delhi, with an exceptional personal-verification route via the Honorary Consulate in Colombo and subsequent filing through VFS in India. The exact route may depend on visa type and current consular arrangements. **Do not promise that VFS Colombo accepts a D visa or that the Honorary Consulate decides applications.** Before booking or sending documents, obtain written confirmation from the Croatian Embassy/Consular Section on whether a Sri Lankan resident's D application can be lodged at VFS Colombo, must be lodged in New Delhi, or follows the special verification process.

Embassy contact published by Croatian MFA: crocons.new-delhi@mvep.hr / croemb.new-delhi@mvep.hr. The official New Delhi mission page lists the consular section client hours as Monday–Friday 10:00–12:00; confirm before contact.

## 8. Fees and post-study
MUP / EU Immigration Portal list temporary-stay administration fee €46.45, biometric card production €31.85 (normal) or €59.73 (accelerated), plus €9.29 card issuance fee. These figures are subject to current fee schedules and must be reconfirmed before payment. The D-visa fee and any VFS service/courier charge are separate; do not quote a fee from another country or from a short-stay C-visa page as the D-visa fee.

The European Commission's Croatia student page (dated 15 September 2025) describes a one-year post-study stay, renewable for one more year, to seek work or establish a business. Because this is an older EU portal summary and the current MUP page reviewed here does not independently confirm the post-study route, keep this **unconfirmed pending current Croatian statutory/MUP verification**. Do not promise it to a client until reviewed against the current Aliens Act and current MUP instructions.

## 9. Counsellor decision checklist
1. Confirm the institution is a recognised Croatian higher-education institution and the student has formal enrolment.
2. Confirm course level, duration, study mode, language, tuition and payment/refund terms with the institution.
3. Calculate funds using the current MUP percentage and salary base: €362.25/month (2026 calculation) plus €869.40 return funds; verify acceptable evidence.
4. Obtain current health-insurance instructions and the correct criminal-record certificate/legalisation/translation checklist.
5. Confirm whether the student applies at the Croatian mission or qualifies for the university-study police-station exception while legally in Croatia.
6. Confirm the Sri Lanka-specific D-visa filing channel directly with the Croatian Embassy; do not assume VFS Colombo.
7. Explain the 20-hour weekly work ceiling and that employment is not guaranteed.
8. Explain the biometric permit, registration deadlines and health-insurance follow-up based on the student's actual route.
9. Do not promise a post-study stay until current legislation is confirmed.
10. Keep this record **Pending human review** until a designated immigration reviewer signs off.
