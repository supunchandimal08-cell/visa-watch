## Counsellor rule notes

### 1. Choose the correct visa category and school

- F-1 is generally for academic study at a U.S. college/university, high school, private elementary school, seminary, conservatory or language-training programme.
- M-1 is generally for vocational or other recognised non-academic study, excluding language training.
- The first step is acceptance by a school certified by the Student and Exchange Visitor Program (SEVP) for the relevant F/M programme. Check the exact legal school and location in the official School Search tool; do not rely on an agent's claim or an accreditation logo alone.
- After acceptance, the school enters the student in SEVIS and issues Form I-20. The student must check the personal details, programme, SEVIS ID, costs and start date. The student and school official sign the I-20.
- A visitor visa is not a substitute for an F/M visa for degree or certificate study.

### 2. Financial evidence — no universal national minimum

There is no single universal U.S. student-visa bank balance to quote to every applicant. Start with the student's Form I-20 and the school's cost-of-attendance estimate, including tuition, living costs, health insurance where charged, books/fees, dependants and travel. The consular officer may request evidence showing how the full costs will be paid. Review the source, availability and consistency of funds, sponsor relationship and sponsor capacity. Do not promise that a fixed bank balance guarantees a visa or rely on future unauthorised work to fund the course.

### 3. Application and interview

1. Obtain acceptance from an SEVP-certified school and the current Form I-20.
2. Pay the I-901 SEVIS fee where required, using the SEVIS ID on the current I-20; retain the receipt.
3. Complete the DS-160 online and retain/print the barcode confirmation page.
4. Follow U.S. Embassy Colombo appointment and payment instructions.
5. Prepare passport, DS-160 confirmation, I-20 signed by student and school official, fee receipts, academic records and credible evidence of funds. Be ready to explain course choice, academic preparation, funding and plans after authorised study.
6. Attend the interview and provide fingerprints or additional documents as instructed. Administrative processing may follow.
7. A visa permits travel to a U.S. port of entry; CBP makes the admission decision. A visa is not a guarantee of entry.

New F/M students may be issued a visa up to 365 days before the programme start date but generally cannot enter more than 30 days before the start date. Continuing students have different entry timing rules; check current State Department guidance and the student's I-20.

### 4. Fees — distinguish the two charges

- State Department nonimmigrant visa application fee: currently USD 185 for F/M categories.
- DHS/ICE I-901 SEVIS fee: currently USD 350 for F/M students.
- These are separate payments. The I-901 fee is normally paid after the I-20 is issued and before the visa interview. Confirm the correct SEVIS ID on the receipt and the current fee/exemption rules before payment. Do not assume a school's administrative fee includes the federal SEVIS fee.
- Fees may change; verify the live official fee schedule on the date of payment.

### 5. Sri Lanka — Colombo post instructions

The U.S. Embassy Colombo post-specific page (last updated 1 September 2026 when checked) instructs applicants, after receiving an interview appointment, to:
- register for document delivery before the interview; and
- schedule a medical examination in Sri Lanka with a designated physician before the interview.

Use only the current Embassy panel-physician list and the applicant's interview instructions. If the Embassy requests additional documents under 221(g), follow the current sealed-envelope or courier instructions. Do not assume the local medical or document-return process is the same as another country.

### 6. F-1 employment and post-study training

- On-campus work is generally limited to 20 hours per week while school is in session; full-time work may be allowed during eligible official breaks.
- Off-campus employment is not a general student benefit. It requires a specific regulatory basis and prior authorisation.
- CPT must be an integral part of the established curriculum and authorised by the DSO in SEVIS before employment starts. Do not start work before the CPT authorisation is recorded on the I-20.
- OPT is temporary practical training directly related to the student's major. The student needs the DSO recommendation and the appropriate USCIS approval/EAD before working. Standard post-completion OPT is generally up to 12 months; eligible STEM graduates may apply for a 24-month extension if they meet the degree, employer and training-plan conditions.
- M-1 practical training has different rules. Do not apply F-1 CPT/OPT assumptions to M-1 students.
- Do not promise a job, earnings, work placement or permanent residence.
