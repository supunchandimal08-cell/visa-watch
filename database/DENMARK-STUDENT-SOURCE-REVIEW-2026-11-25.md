# Denmark Student Residence Permit — Official Source Review (25 November 2026)

## Scope
For Sri Lankan non-EU/EEA applicants seeking higher education in Denmark. Denmark has an opt-out from EU immigration and student residence rules; use Danish SIRI/New to Denmark rules, not a generic EU/Schengen student visa summary. For studies over 90 days, a Danish residence permit is required before arrival. All VisaWatch legal entries remain Pending human review.

## 1. Admission and programme eligibility
- Applicant must be admitted to a full-time higher educational programme in Denmark. Part-time study is not eligible under the ordinary higher-education student route (specific working-professional master's arrangements have their own rules).
- Programme must be approved by a Danish state authority. If not state-approved, it must have a valid advisory statement from the Secretariat to the Accreditation Council. Since 15 September 2026, that secretariat took over from the Danish Evaluation Institute (EVA); old EVA statements issued before 1 July 2026 may remain valid while in force.
- Applicant must speak and understand the language of instruction sufficiently to actively participate. Institution/course admission conditions and SIRI permit eligibility are separate checks.
- Higher-education routes include academy profession, professional bachelor's, bachelor's, master's, certain part-time professional master's and exchange/guest study. Check the exact SIRI category.

## 2. 2026 financial self-support — important tuition-paid exception
- 2026 self-support benchmark: DKK 7,426 per month. For study periods longer than one year, funds are normally required for up to 12 months (maximum DKK 89,112).
- If completing a full higher-education programme in Denmark and the applicant has paid tuition in Denmark, SIRI says separate proof of self-support is not required under this route. Do not automatically request DKK 89,112 from every tuition-paying full-degree applicant.
- If tuition is waived/free and the applicant is not receiving a Danish state scholarship covering both tuition and living expenses, they must document a scholarship covering living expenses or disposable funds of DKK 7,426/month for the study period (maximum 12 months).
- Exchange/guest students must show scholarship or disposable funds of DKK 7,426/month for the period in Denmark. Basic/youth programme students also need funds. Evidence may include a bank statement in the applicant's name, showing cash funds and currency, or scholarship/grant evidence.
- Family members, where eligible to apply, require an additional DKK 7,426 per person per month, up to 12 months.
- Crypto assets are not accepted as financial evidence. A private sponsor letter is not a substitute for the applicant's own disposable funds or an eligible scholarship; follow the exact SIRI evidence rules.

## 3. Work rights
- Students in a state-approved higher educational programme receive a limited work permit with the student residence permit: up to 90 hours per month, and full-time in June, July and August.
- The residence card states whether and to what extent the student may work. Exceeding the limit or working without permission can result in warning, revocation/refusal of extension, deportation and penalties for the employer.
- Students in non-state-approved higher educational programmes who applied on or after 2 May 2025 are not granted a work permit under this student route. Older transitional cases may differ.
- A working-professional master's programme has a separate limited permit for relevant part-time employment with a specified employer. Do not apply the general 90-hour rule without checking the specific permit category.

## 4. Major reforms effective 1 October 2026 — application date matters
- For relevant third-country students admitted to state-approved higher educational programmes, applications submitted on or after 1 October 2026 generally no longer allow spouse/registered partner/cohabiting partner or children to receive accompanying-family residence permits under the student rules. PhD students are not affected; special Turkish Association Agreement rules may apply.
- For eligible state-approved degree students applying on or after 1 October 2026, post-study job-seeking residence permit is generally 6 months or 1 year depending on the degree/programme category, rather than the former up-to-3-year period. PhD graduates generally retain a 3-year job-seeking period.
- Applications submitted before 1 October 2026 follow transition rules. For spouses/partners, the family member's own application also generally had to be submitted before 1 October 2026 to qualify under old rules; children under 18 may have a later filing possibility if the student's application was submitted before the cutoff. Check each family member's date and exact category.
- The new rules do not affect every student route in the same way. PhD, Turkish Association Agreement, non-state-approved programmes and working-professional master's may have distinct rules. Never state a blanket family ban or blanket one-year job-search entitlement without checking category and filing date.

## 5. Application steps, documents, fees and timing
- Create a SIRI case order ID, pay the 2026 study-case processing fee of DKK 3,060, gather evidence, and complete the ST1 application. The educational institution completes its part first; the applicant then completes their part after the institution's email invitation.
- Common documents: receipt for paid SIRI fee; copy of all passport pages including blank pages and covers; proof of admission/enrolment; evidence of tuition payment where relevant; self-support evidence where required; and any programme-specific documents. If under 18, parental consent, birth certificate and institution declaration are required.
- After online submission, biometrics must normally be recorded within 14 days. Bring original passport and application confirmation. Processing time for a higher-education application is normally 2 months; can be up to 3 months if further information is needed. These are published normal times, not guarantees.
- Sri Lanka route: Denmark is represented for all Schengen and residence-permit applications from Sri Lanka by the Royal Norwegian Embassy in New Delhi. The official Danish Embassy India page says residence-permit applications from Sri Lanka are lodged through the Norwegian Visa Application Centre (VFS Global) in Colombo; the case is handled by the Norwegian Embassy in New Delhi. Do not send student residence applicants to Danish Embassy New Delhi or assume a Danish VFS in Colombo.
- Verify the current Norwegian Embassy/VFS appointment process, biometrics, any VFS service fee and document checklist before the applicant travels. The online SIRI application and local biometric/identity steps are separate.

## 6. Post-study pathway
- Completion of an entire state-approved higher educational programme in Denmark may qualify for a job-seeking residence permit. For applications from 1 October 2026, relevant academy profession, professional bachelor's, bachelor's and master's degree graduates generally receive 6 months or 1 year depending on the category. PhD graduates generally receive 3 years.
- The job-seeking permit and its duration are category-specific; do not promise a 3-year post-study stay to a new Bachelor's/Master's applicant. The student work permit may continue during the job-seeking period, but the residence card and exact conditions control.
- This is not a job guarantee. A later employment-based residence/work permit requires meeting the relevant Danish scheme and conditions.

## Counsellor checklist
1. Confirm the exact qualification, institution, state approval/advisory statement, full-time status, language of instruction and study category.
2. Confirm whether tuition is paid, waived, scholarship-funded or exchange-based before asking for DKK 7,426/month evidence.
3. If funds are required, calculate DKK 7,426 × months (maximum 12 months / DKK 89,112 for stays over a year). Verify applicant's own disposable funds or eligible scholarship; crypto is not accepted.
4. Explain work permit only after confirming state-approved status and exact category: 90 hours/month plus full-time June-August for eligible state-approved higher education.
5. Check the 1 October 2026 application cutoff before discussing accompanying family or job-search duration. Record the exact date each applicant applied.
6. For Sri Lanka, confirm Norwegian VFS Colombo route and Norwegian Embassy New Delhi processing; complete online SIRI steps and biometrics within the deadline.
7. Explain DKK 3,060 SIRI fee and normal 2-month processing (up to 3 months if more information is needed); never guarantee a decision date.
8. Keep all legal records Pending human review until authorised reviewer approval.
