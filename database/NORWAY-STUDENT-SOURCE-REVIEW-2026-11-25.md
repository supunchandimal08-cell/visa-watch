# Norway Student Residence Permit — Official Source Review (25 November 2026)

## Scope
Sri Lankan citizens seeking full-time higher education in Norway for more than 90 days. The responsible immigration authority is the Norwegian Directorate of Immigration (UDI). This is a national Norwegian study residence permit, not a single EU student visa. All VisaWatch rules remain Pending human review.

## 1. Eligibility and admission
- For university/university-college study, applicant must have an admission offer, study full-time and attend an institution on the Norwegian Agency for Quality Assurance in Education (NOKUT) approved/accredited list.
- Degree admission also has academic entry requirements. The general basis is the Higher Education Entrance Qualification (GSU); country-specific education requirements and programme-specific academic/language requirements apply. Check Sri Lanka's current GSU entry before making an eligibility statement.
- Applicant must have a place to live in Norway and demonstrate funds for the study period.
- UDI assesses whether the circumstances in the applicant's home country indicate that the applicant is likely to return home after studies. This is a substantive assessment, not a guaranteed outcome.
- Online-only courses are not eligible under the vocational-school route; check the exact programme category.
- More than 3 months in Norway requires a study residence permit. Normally applicant cannot enter Norway until the application has been processed; no work, including remote work, before the permit is granted.

## 2. 2026/2027 living-funds requirement
- UDI and Study in Norway currently state NOK 15,488 per month / NOK 170,368 for a full academic year (2026–2027).
- For one semester: NOK 77,440 for autumn or NOK 92,928 for spring.
- Funds may consist of student loans, grants, the applicant's own funds in a Norwegian bank account, or a deposit account held by the educational institution. If the applicant has no Norwegian bank account, ask the institution whether its deposit account arrangement is available.
- A confirmed part-time job offer may be included in the funding calculation where UDI allows it; do not advise a student to rely on a hoped-for job.
- Tuition fees are additional. If tuition is payable, applicant must show they can cover it or that it has been paid; institution must confirm any tuition exemption. Follow the current academic year's invoice and UDI instructions, not old tuition figures.
- Semester fee is approximately NOK 1,000 per semester to the student welfare organisation, separate from living funds and tuition. Actual amount is institution-specific.

## 3. Tuition fees and course costs
- Degree-seeking students from outside EU/EEA and Switzerland normally pay tuition fees at Norwegian institutions; amounts vary by institution and programme.
- Exchange students, doctoral candidates and some named scholarship/exchange schemes are normally exempt at public institutions. Confirm the exact student's exemption in writing with the institution.
- Norway does not offer one universal government scholarship open to all international students. Check institution-specific scholarships and eligibility; do not advertise a general government-funded scholarship.
- Budget separately for tuition, living funds, semester fee, travel, insurance and application/VFS fees.

## 4. Work rights during studies
- A granted study permit automatically includes permission to work up to 20 hours per week during the semester, including remote work, and full-time during holidays.
- Students cannot be self-employed or run their own business on the basis of the study permit.
- Work may not start until the study permit is granted. Do not promise a job, hours beyond the permit, or earnings sufficient to cover all costs.
- Check the wording of the actual residence permit and any later UDI conditions.

## 5. Family members
- For a university/university-college student, spouse/cohabitant and children can usually apply for family immigration, subject to separate eligibility, fees and documentation.
- UDI currently states it does not have capacity to process family members' applications at the same time as the student's application in the ordinary student route. Do not promise simultaneous decisions.
- Other categories, such as folk high school and certain school routes, may not permit accompanying family. Confirm the exact study category.

## 6. Post-study job-seeker permit
- A person who completed their degree/education in Norway on a qualifying study permit may apply for a job-seeker residence permit before the current permit expires; UDI advises applying at least one month before expiry.
- The applicant must seek skilled employment and meet the qualification criteria. They must have completed their Norwegian education; if diploma/transcript is not yet issued, a formal institution confirmation may be accepted if it confirms thesis/final exams and completion of all other credits.
- 2026 UDI maintenance requirement for this job-seeker route: NOK 28,448/month or NOK 341,373/year. Funds must be the applicant's own and are usually required in a Norwegian bank account. Qualifying job income may be included under UDI rules.
- Permit can be granted for a maximum of one year. During this period the graduate may work full-time or part-time, including in jobs not requiring skilled-worker qualifications, while looking for relevant skilled employment.
- This is not an automatic extension, permanent-residence route or job guarantee. Study permit time does not count toward permanent residence.

## 7. Application fees and Sri Lanka application route
- UDI fee for first-time study permit for an applicant over 18: NOK 5,400. Under 18: NOK 2,700. Confirm the live fee page before payment.
- Register the application online in UDI's Application Portal and pay the UDI fee before the appointment. Print and sign the application summary/signature letter and attach the payment receipt.
- For Sri Lanka, applications are lodged at Norway Visa Application Centre (VFS Global), Browns Capital Building, 3rd Floor, No. 19 Dudley Senanayake Mawatha, Colombo 08. Appointment required. VFS forwards the application to the Norwegian Embassy in New Delhi for screening/forwarding to UDI.
- The Norway in Sri Lanka mission page still lists older charges of EUR 17 service fee plus EUR 5.50 courier (July 2026). The current VFS Norway Sri Lanka page lists EUR 20 service fee (LKR 7,080), EUR 18 passport-resubmission fee if requested, and LKR 4,950 mandated fee for Sri Lankan passport holders. This is a source discrepancy; verify exact live charges at booking and do not add optional passport resubmission automatically.
- Applicant must be a Sri Lankan citizen or hold a Sri Lankan residence permit. The Norway in Sri Lanka page also states a Sri Lankan residence permit must have been held for at least six months before applying for a Schengen permit; confirm applicability if applicant is not a Sri Lankan citizen.
- Gather the UDI-generated checklist. Common documents include passport, signed cover letter, admission confirmation, proof of accommodation, funds evidence, tuition payment/exemption evidence and any institution-specific forms. Additional documents/interview may be requested.
- Do not travel before decision if an entry visa is required. After approval, the applicant may need an entry visa sticker to travel; follow the Embassy/UDI instruction.

## 8. After-arrival residence card
- Once the student knows the arrival date, they should book the police appointment for the residence card. The Norway in Sri Lanka mission says the appointment should normally be within the first seven days after arrival or the first available appointment.
- Follow the exact instruction in the UDI decision letter and attend the correct police/SUA office. Keep passport, entry visa (if required) and permit decision available.
- This is a post-arrival step and is separate from the initial VFS Colombo application.
- UDI confirms that part-time work, including remote work, cannot begin until the study permit has been granted.

## Counsellor checklist
1. Verify institution accreditation, full-time admission, course mode, GSU country requirements, academic prerequisites and English/Norwegian language proof.
2. Calculate NOK 170,368 for a full 2026/27 academic year (or relevant semester amount); keep tuition and semester fee separate.
3. Confirm the exact bank/deposit account route with the institution. Do not rely on future part-time work as the main funding plan.
4. Explain work: up to 20 hours/week during study and full-time during holidays, only after permit grant; no self-employment.
5. Check housing proof, family intentions and family application timing.
6. Explain NOK 5,400 adult UDI fee separately from VFS service/courier fees; verify live amounts.
7. Direct Sri Lankan applicants to VFS Colombo; processing is through Norwegian Embassy New Delhi and UDI. Book appointment and submit UDI checklist.
8. For post-study plans, explain maximum one-year job-seeker route, skilled-job focus and separate NOK 28,448/month maintenance requirement.
9. Explain the police residence-card appointment after arrival; follow the decision letter and book within the first seven days or first available slot.
10. Keep every legal rule Pending human review until an authorised reviewer approves it.

## 11. Official-source recheck — 26 November 2026

### 2026/2027 university funds and tuition
- UDI's Sri Lanka-personalised study permit page currently states **NOK 15,488/month / NOK 170,368/year** for university/university college study. One semester is NOK 77,440 (autumn) or NOK 92,928 (spring). Funds can be grants, loans, own funds in a Norwegian bank account or an institution deposit account; a confirmed part-time job offer may be counted where UDI allows it, but never budget expected or hoped-for work income.
- The Study in Norway national portal confirms the 2026/27 living-cost amount, the approximate NOK 1,000 semester fee, and that non-EU/EEA/Swiss degree-seeking students normally pay tuition fees that vary by institution and programme. Exchange students, doctoral candidates and specified scholarship/exchange schemes may be exempt at public institutions.
- The personalised UDI page still contains an old reference to tuition fees for academic year 2025/26. For a 2026/27 application, obtain the current invoice or written tuition exemption from the institution and keep tuition separate from living funds.

### Application fee and VFS fee discrepancy
- UDI fee page: study permit adult applicant NOK 5,400; under-18 applicant NOK 2,700.
- Norway in Sri Lanka official page still shows EUR 17 VFS service fee plus EUR 5.50 courier (July 2026). The current VFS Norway Sri Lanka page lists EUR 20 service fee (shown as LKR 7,080), EUR 18 passport resubmission fee if requested, and LKR 4,950 mandated fee for Sri Lankan passport holders.
- Treat the VFS fee discrepancy as unresolved and confirm the live checkout amount before payment. Do not automatically add optional passport resubmission fee if it is not requested.

### Post-study job seeker route
- UDI's current job-seeker page confirms eligible graduates who completed their education in Norway on a qualifying study permit must apply before the current permit expires; UDI advises at least one month before expiry.
- 2026 requirement is NOK 28,448/month or NOK 341,373/year. Funds must be the applicant's own and are usually required in a Norwegian bank account. A qualifying job offer/income may be included under UDI's rules.
- Maximum permit duration is one year; the graduate may work full-time or part-time while seeking relevant skilled employment, but cannot be self-employed or run a business on this permit.

### Sri Lanka route and after-arrival appointment
- Sri Lankan residence-permit applications are submitted at VFS Colombo and forwarded to the Norwegian Embassy in New Delhi, which screens/forwards to UDI. Applicants should use the current UDI checklist generated for their own case.
- After approval, a student who needs an entry visa must wait for the decision and entry sticker. On arrival, book the police appointment for the residence card, normally within the first 7 days or the first available appointment, following the decision letter.
