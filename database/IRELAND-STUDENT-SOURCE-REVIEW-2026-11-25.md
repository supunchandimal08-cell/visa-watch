# Ireland Student Visa and Immigration Review — 25 November 2026

## Scope and review status
This is a counsellor research note for non-EEA applicants resident in Sri Lanka. Ireland is an EU member but is not in the Schengen Area. A short-stay Schengen visa is not an Irish long-stay study visa. All operational and legal summaries below remain **Pending human review**; check the live official source and the applicant's exact course, provider, nationality and filing date before client advice.

## 1. Course and provider eligibility
- For study longer than 90 days, use the Irish long-stay study visa route.
- The course must be eligible under the current student immigration framework: check both the remaining Interim List of Eligible Programmes (ILEP) and the eligible programmes offered by providers authorised to use the TrustEd Ireland statutory quality mark.
- ILEP is closed to new provider applications; its final update was published 20 June 2025. ILEP will be superseded by TrustEd Ireland. Do not assume a provider or course is eligible because it appears on an agent's brochure or a university marketing page.
- The official long-stay acceptance letter must confirm full-time education with at least 15 hours organised daytime tuition weekly. Confirm exact course, award level, provider, campus and current ILEP/TrustEd listing.
- Stamp 2 normally applies to eligible full-time programmes. Stamp 2A is a different permission and does not allow work.

## 2. Tuition and fee payment before visa
- If course fees are €6,000 or less, pay them in full before applying.
- If course fees are more than €6,000, pay at least €6,000 before applying, unless the provider requires full payment earlier.
- Provider terms may require full payment; confirm refund policy, learner protection, deposit and fee schedule in writing.
- These are minimum immigration payment rules, not the full course cost.

## 3. Financial evidence
For visa-required applicants, including Sri Lankan nationals:
- Academic course of more than 8 months / one academic year: show immediate access to at least €10,000 for year one.
- Also show ready access to at least €10,000 for each subsequent year, in addition to the tuition fees for those years.
- Course resulting in stay of 8 months or less: show €833 per month or €6,665 total, whichever is lower.
- The student must be able to support themselves without relying on casual employment.
- Provide six months of personal bank statements, even if a sponsor is paying. Where a sponsor is involved, provide six months of sponsor statements, relationship evidence, sponsor letter, income/employment evidence and proof the sponsor can support themselves and dependants.
- Explain large/irregular deposits and the source of funds. Credit cards are not accepted. Restricted deposit accounts require bank confirmation of access.
- A €10,000 education bond is an alternative for eligible degree-programme students only; confirm approved provider, course eligibility and source of bond funds. It is not a universal alternative for all students.

## 4. Work rights — Stamp 2
- Eligible Stamp 2 students may work casual employment up to 20 hours per week during term time.
- Up to 40 hours per week is permitted during the specified holiday periods: June, July, August and September, and 15 December to 15 January.
- Stamp 2A students cannot work. Stamp 2 students cannot be self-employed or operate a business.
- Work is a limited concession, not the primary purpose of the stay. Do not use anticipated part-time income as proof of funds or promise a job/hours/income.
- Eligible course-integral placements/internships may have different conditions; verify course level, credit, duration, institution approval and current immigration guidance before advising.

## 5. Post-study — Third Level Graduate Programme (Stamp 1G)
- Eligible Irish-educated non-EEA graduates with an NFQ Level 8 award may receive up to 12 months.
- Eligible NFQ Level 9 or above graduates may receive up to 24 months: 12 months initially, with a further 12 months subject to conditions and evidence of efforts to obtain graduate-level employment.
- Applicant must hold current Stamp 2 and a current immigration registration card and apply within six months of being notified of the final award.
- The purpose is to seek graduate-level employment and apply for a General Employment Permit, Critical Skills Employment Permit or research hosting agreement.
- This is not automatic, does not guarantee employment, and does not itself guarantee a longer-term work permission.

## 6. Sri Lanka application route
- The Embassy of Ireland's information leaflet (version 25 April 2025) says the New Delhi Visa Office (NDVO) processes student visa applications from residents of Sri Lanka and other named South Asian countries who have an unconditional offer for an eligible course.
- The leaflet says VFS document submission is available only to residents of India or Nepal; applicants resident elsewhere must follow the application-office instructions. Therefore do not direct Sri Lankan residents to VFS Colombo based on assumptions. Use AVATS and the generated Summary Application Form, then follow the current NDVO instructions for document submission and fee payment.
- The leaflet states applications may be submitted five months before course start; the current Immigration Service Delivery general long-stay page says up to three months before travel. Treat this as a discrepancy: follow the current route-specific instructions and confirm the permitted filing window with NDVO before scheduling.
- The NDVO leaflet states an approved English test is compulsory for student visas. Confirm the currently accepted tests and minimum level for the exact course.
- The long-stay application page requires original supporting documents, certified translations for documents not in English/Irish, and says biometrics may be required; the application office confirms whether/how.
- Do not state a fixed biometrics appointment location or courier address without checking the live AVATS summary and NDVO instruction.

## 7. Fees, registration and arrival
- Irish entry visa fee: €60 single-entry or €100 multiple-entry; additional local submission/service charges may apply. Check fee exemption and the payment instructions on the AVATS summary.
- Non-EEA nationals staying more than 90 days generally must register their immigration permission after arrival. Current registration fee is €300, subject to exemptions and current instructions.
- Students must register for the correct permission (normally Stamp 2 for eligible study) and comply with the permission expiry/renewal requirements. Stamp 2 is not reckonable residence for naturalisation.
- Private medical insurance is required for the student route; confirm the policy meets current requirements and whether the provider includes it.

## 8. Family members
Non-EEA students on Stamp 2 generally cannot bring family members to Ireland as dependants. Family members usually need to qualify for and apply for permission in their own right; rare exceptions must be checked with Immigration Service Delivery. Do not promise dependant visas.

## 9. Counsellor checklist
1. Confirm nationality and country of legal residence.
2. Verify exact provider and course on the current ILEP and TrustEd eligible-programme lists.
3. Confirm full-time status, at least 15 organised daytime tuition hours weekly, course award level and start date.
4. Obtain unconditional offer and fee schedule; confirm €6,000/full-fee prepayment rule and refund policy.
5. Calculate funds: €10,000 year one plus €10,000 per subsequent year, tuition and travel/insurance as relevant; use €833/month or €6,665 cap for eligible courses of 8 months or less.
6. Review six-month personal and sponsor statements, income, relationship evidence and unusual deposits.
7. Check English test, academic progression, study gaps, statement of purpose, passport validity, insurance and previous visa refusals.
8. Generate AVATS application and follow the exact submission route stated in its summary and current NDVO instructions; never assume VFS Colombo.
9. Explain Stamp 2 work limits, Stamp 2A no-work rule, no self-employment and no job guarantee.
10. Explain registration, €300 registration fee, family restrictions and eligible Stamp 1G rules without guaranteeing future work permission.
