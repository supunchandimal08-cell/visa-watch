# Finland Student Residence Permit — Official Source Review (25 November 2026)

## Scope
For non-EU/EEA applicants who intend to study in Finland. A stay of more than 90 days normally requires a residence permit for studies; for 90 days or less, a visa may be required depending on nationality. First residence permit is applied for abroad. Eligibility is tied to a qualifying study place and the nature of the programme. Migri is the deciding authority. All VisaWatch records remain Pending human review.

## 1. Study place and permit
- Applicant must be accepted by a Finnish educational institution. The study must lead to a degree or vocational qualification, or be an exchange programme; some other studies may qualify only with a well-founded reason.
- Eligible institutions include universities, universities of applied sciences, general upper secondary schools and vocational institutions.
- A permit is not granted for studies mainly consisting of distance learning that do not require the student to stay in Finland.
- First permit is applied for from abroad; extended permit is applied for in Finland.
- Higher-education degree students are generally granted a continuous A permit; other study routes and exchange students may receive a temporary B permit. Permit type affects later residence pathways.

## 2. Living funds and tuition — official 2026 guidance checked
- Minimum living funds: €800 per month. For a one-year stay, the applicant must show €9,600 in their own available funds at application. For a shorter stay, calculate €800 for each month.
- The first-year evidence normally includes a bank statement covering the previous six months. The account must be in the applicant's own name and for their personal use; it must be accessible in Finland.
- A private sponsorship agreement or a promise from a relative/friend/employer is not accepted as proof by itself. If money was transferred by parents or relatives, Migri may request evidence of the source, such as sponsor bank statements or tax documents. Do not describe a family sponsorship letter alone as satisfying the funds rule.
- Tuition is separate from the €800/month living funds. If tuition has not been paid before applying, the applicant must already have enough money in the account to cover the tuition as well as living funds. Living funds cannot be counted again for tuition.
- Scholarships/grants for living costs can reduce the amount of personal funds required if supported by official evidence. If the institution provides free accommodation, the amount may reduce to €400/month; if it provides both free accommodation and meals, €270/month. Obtain institution's written confirmation.
- On a first application, the applicant cannot rely on future work in Finland to meet the income requirement.

## 3. Health insurance
- Student must have private insurance covering medical and pharmaceutical expenses, valid on arrival and for the required period. Excess/deductible must not exceed €300.
- If studies last less than two years: insurance must cover medical expenses of at least €120,000.
- If studies last two years or longer: insurance must cover pharmaceutical expenses of at least €40,000.
- Ordinary travel insurance limited to trips of 90 days is not accepted. Migri assesses policy terms individually. EHIC/UK GHIC or a Kela card can remove the need for separate insurance only if valid for the stay.

## 4. Application route, documents and fee
- Submit the first application online through Enter Finland, upload required documents and pay the processing fee. Then book an appointment at the Finnish mission or authorised service provider designated for the applicant's country to prove identity and give fingerprints. The application is processed after identity verification.
- Sri Lankan applicants must use the Finnish mission / service provider designated by Finland's Ministry for Foreign Affairs for their country of residence. Do not guess a VFS centre or mission from another country; confirm the current location and appointment channel on Finland Abroad before the client books travel.
- Common attachments: valid passport, recent passport photo, colour copy of passport bio page, acceptance/attendance certificate, six-month bank statement, proof of tuition payment or scholarship, insurance certificate, and any case-specific supporting documents. Non-Finnish/Swedish/English documents may need authorised translation and legalisation.
- Current adult first-application fee displayed by Migri: €600 online / €750 paper. Fees can change; verify at filing. A 100-day D visa can be requested alongside the residence permit in qualifying cases, but only if the applicant can travel within two weeks after the permit is granted because the residence card must be collected in Finland.
- Apply early after acceptance. Do not promise a processing time or buy non-refundable flights before a decision/card and entry arrangements are confirmed.

## 5. Work while studying
- With a study residence permit, paid work in any field is allowed for an average of 30 hours per week, calculated over the year (up to 1,560 hours per year). Some weeks may exceed 30 hours if the annual average remains within the limit.
- Degree-related practical training and diploma/thesis work may be outside the 30-hour limit only when the work is agreed as part of the degree and earns credits under the applicable rules.
- Work must not slow down studies; the student must make normal academic progress. Never promise a job, income or that part-time work will cover tuition/living costs.

## 6. Renewal and academic progress
- Apply for an extended permit in Finland before the current permit expires. Migri may ask for transcript/credit records, evidence of continuing study, tuition payment and updated funds/insurance.
- Migri's student renewal instructions state that if the student has fewer than 45 credits from the previous academic year, they should attach an explanation of studies and future plans. This is not a universal automatic refusal threshold; individual circumstances are assessed.

## 7. After graduation
- Eligible graduates who have completed a degree in Finland may apply for a residence permit to look for work or start a business. The permit can be granted for a maximum of two years and may be used in parts subject to minimum and final-date conditions.
- The application must be made within five years after the student/research residence permit expired. Funds of €800/month are required for the job-search permit; eligible extended-permit applicants may in some circumstances rely on work income.
- A job-search permit gives unrestricted right to work, but is not a job guarantee or automatic permanent residence. A separate work-based permit or other qualifying residence route may be needed later.

## Counsellor checklist
1. Confirm nationality, country of residence, course length, programme type, institution and whether the programme requires physical attendance in Finland.
2. Verify acceptance letter, degree/vocational outcome, tuition amount, scholarship and start date.
3. Calculate €800 × months (normally €9,600 for one year); add unpaid tuition separately.
4. Check six-month personal bank statement and evidence/source of large family transfers. Do not rely on a sponsor letter alone.
5. Check insurance coverage against course duration: €120,000 medical under two years; €40,000 pharmaceutical for two years or longer; deductible max €300.
6. Confirm current Finnish mission / VFS identity-verification location for Sri Lankan residents on Finland Abroad before booking.
7. Explain the 30-hour weekly average, 1,560 annual hours and degree-credit internship exception carefully.
8. Explain academic progress, renewal and the separate maximum two-year post-study job-search permit. No job or approval guarantee.
9. Keep all legal entries Pending human review until the authorised reviewer approves them.
