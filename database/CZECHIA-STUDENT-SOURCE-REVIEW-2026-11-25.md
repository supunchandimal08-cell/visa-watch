# Czechia — student visa and residence source review
**Review date:** 25 November 2026  
**Jurisdiction:** Czech Republic / Czechia  
**Applicant lens:** Sri Lankan citizen applying from Sri Lanka  
**Record status:** Pending human review — not legal advice

## 1. Correct immigration route
For a third-country national undertaking a qualifying study programme under Section 64 of Act No. 326/1999 Coll., the main long-stay route is the **Long-term Residence Permit for the Purpose of Studies** (stay over 90 days). Qualifying programmes include an accredited university study programme, certain university-run language/professional preparation courses, and secondary/higher vocational study as part of an exchange programme. Not every language course, private training course or short course qualifies.

The applicant can apply directly for the long-term residence permit at the competent Czech diplomatic mission, or may apply for a long-term visa for the purpose of studies where that route fits the actual programme and stay. These are distinct legal routes. If the residence permit is approved abroad, the mission issues a single-entry D/VR visa for the purpose of collecting the residence permit (unless the person can enter legally without it). D/VR is not itself the long-term residence card.

## 2. Sri Lanka application route — New Delhi
The Czech Embassy in New Delhi has territorial jurisdiction for Sri Lanka and accepts Sri Lankan citizens' long-term visa and long-term residence applications. The applicant must submit in person, and the Embassy's appointment instructions are published separately for study applicants from Sri Lanka and the other countries in its jurisdiction.

The Embassy's 2026 study appointment page specified timed email registration windows and a randomized assignment process. The listed 2026 dates on the page (June–September 2026) have already passed as at this review date. Do **not** reuse those dates or imply walk-in availability. Check the Embassy's latest study appointment notice before each application cycle. The published 2026 notice instructed eligible applicants to email newdelhi.visa@mzv.gov.cz during the specified window, with the passport number alone as subject and PDF copies of passport biodata and admission/study confirmation; one email per applicant. This is a historic process example, not a current appointment date.

The Embassy's current long-stay information page says applications are submitted in person at the Embassy of the Czech Republic in New Delhi. Short-stay Schengen arrangements via other embassies or VFS do not apply automatically to long-stay study cases. Confirm current registration window, exact documents and any changed process directly with the Embassy.

## 3. Eligibility and document checklist
For a direct long-term residence application from outside Czechia, the official Information Portal for Foreigners lists:
- completed application form and valid travel document;
- proof of accommodation;
- proof of funds for the residence;
- photograph;
- comprehensive medical insurance;
- original confirmation of studies / enrolment;
- criminal-record-equivalent document issued by the applicant's country of citizenship (not required for applicants under 15);
- parental consent if under 18.

A medical report and criminal-record documents from other countries where the applicant resided for a total of six months during the preceding three years may be requested. Documents must meet formal requirements; foreign public documents may require higher verification and official Czech translation. The New Delhi Embassy specifically says submitted documents must be in Czech or officially translated into Czech, and foreign public documents must be apostilled or superlegalised as applicable. Verify Sri Lankan document chain and translation format with the Embassy before obtaining expensive certifications.

The school confirmation should identify the institution, applicant, citizenship, date of birth, expected stay, programme name/type, study mode and, where relevant, exchange programme. Confirm the provider and exact programme are an accredited/qualifying route under Section 64 before collecting funds or visa fees.

## 4. Financial means — current official calculation base
The Czech Ministry of Labour and Social Affairs page was updated 1 October 2026 and confirms the subsistence minimum (existenční minimum) remains **CZK 3,130 per month**. The Czech Ministry of Interior's finance guidance uses this amount for long-term residence applications: **15 × CZK 3,130 + 2 × CZK 3,130 for each full month after the first** of the intended stay.

Examples:
- 6-month stay: 15 × 3,130 + 5 × 2 × 3,130 = **CZK 78,250**.
- 12-month stay: 15 × 3,130 + 11 × 2 × 3,130 = **CZK 115,810**.

These are statutory financial-evidence calculations, not a realistic all-in cost-of-living budget. Accommodation and tuition must be budgeted separately unless the school/sponsor is covering them and the authority accepts the evidence. The official finance guidance also permits qualifying study applicants to submit a commitment from a public authority/legal entity to provide funds equal to the subsistence minimum per month, proof that the receiving organisation/school covers all study-related costs, or an eligible international-agreement grant; if the commitment does not cover the required amount, the applicant may need to evidence the difference under the official rule. Confirm the exact evidence route with the Ministry/Embassy for the applicant's study category. Applicants under 18 show half the stated amount.

Evidence may include an account statement in the applicant's name and a valid internationally recognised payment card; the authority may request the card or further account evidence. Do not rely on anticipated part-time earnings to satisfy the visa funds requirement.

## 5. Permit validity, processing and arrival
The study residence permit may be valid for up to **one year**, or up to **two years in cases provided by law**. Accredited university students can repeatedly extend for up to two years at a time, provided they continue qualifying studies. University-run language/professional preparation may be extended only once. Other study-visit/volunteering cases may not be extendable under this route.

The official processing period for an overseas study residence application is **60 days**, but it may pause if documents are missing or proceedings are suspended. The consular fee shown by the Ministry of Interior/MFA is **CZK 2,500**; verify current local-currency payment and any exemption with the Embassy. The permit extension application is due no earlier than 120 days before expiry and no later than the last day of validity.

After approval, the mission issues the D/VR entry visa where needed. Before visa issuance, the applicant must show travel medical insurance for emergency/essential care for the first 90 days plus comprehensive insurance for the rest, or comprehensive insurance for the whole authorised stay (subject to legal exemptions). After arrival, a D/VR visa holder must register at a Ministry of Interior office within **30 calendar days**; a person who can enter without D/VR must register within **3 days**. Biometrics are then taken and the biometric residence card collected. This is the residence-permit route. A person issued a separate long-term study visa (not D/VR) must register with the Foreign Police within **3 business days**; do not mix the two registration deadlines.

## 6. Work rights during study
A third-country national studying a **full-time accredited university programme** in Czechia has free access to the Czech labour market under the Employment Act. This means no separate work permit is required for employment while the qualifying study status continues. This is not a blanket entitlement for every language, preparatory, exchange, part-time or non-accredited course. The student must maintain the qualifying full-time study status and valid residence permission. The rule is not a job guarantee or a promise of hours or income. Confirm the programme's accreditation and study mode before counselling.

## 7. Post-study route
A graduate who holds a study-purpose long-term residence permit and has completed a **university-accredited study programme in Czechia** may apply before the current permit expires for a long-term residence permit to seek employment or start a business. The permit is valid for up to **9 months and cannot be extended**. It grants free access to the labour market, allowing the eligible graduate to work or seek employment without limitation during its validity. It is not available merely because someone completed any private course or language course; the qualifying university programme and current residence status must be confirmed.

## 8. Tuition and admission
The current official Study in Czechia portal states that degree programmes taught in Czech at public and state higher-education institutions are tuition-free regardless of nationality, subject to possible admission/administrative fees and exceptions for extending study beyond the standard period. Programmes taught in English or another foreign language generally charge tuition; exact fees are set by the university and programme. Private institutions set their own fees. Do not quote one national tuition figure. Confirm the exact programme, language, award, entry requirements, recognition of Sri Lankan qualifications, language test, tuition and refund terms directly with the university.

## 9. Counsellor verification checklist
1. Verify the university and exact programme are accredited/qualifying under Section 64; distinguish a degree from a language course or private training.
2. Confirm full-time study status if the client expects student work access.
3. Confirm direct long-term residence vs long-term study visa route with the school and Czech Embassy.
4. For a Sri Lankan citizen, use the Czech Embassy New Delhi route; check the latest appointment notice, not old registration dates.
5. Confirm original admission confirmation, accommodation, passport, photo, criminal-record documents, insurance and Czech translation/legalisation.
6. Calculate funds from CZK 3,130 current subsistence minimum and intended stay length; record the calculation and funding evidence.
7. Explain the 60-day statutory processing period can pause; no visa or permit is guaranteed.
8. Explain free labour-market access only for qualifying full-time accredited university study; no job/income guarantee.
9. Explain the 9-month post-study route only to eligible graduates of Czech-accredited university programmes.
