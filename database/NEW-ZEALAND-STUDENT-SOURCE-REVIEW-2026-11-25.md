## Counsellor rule notes

### 1. Admission and student visa eligibility

- Obtain an offer of place from an approved education provider. Confirm the exact provider and programme, campus and NZQCF level, and check the provider's current NZQA Code of Practice signatory status.
- Check tuition payment for 1 year or the programme (whichever is shorter), or whether the applicant qualifies for the approval-in-principle option and its deadlines.
- Applicant must show adequate living funds, outward travel, acceptable insurance, genuine intentions, health and character, and other visa-specific requirements.
- INZ recommends applying at least 3 months before intended travel. The visa may be issued for a period shorter than the full programme, including where passport expiry limits its duration.
- Student visa holders must attend the course on their visa and make satisfactory academic progress. Partner and dependent children do not get included in the Fee Paying Student Visa application; they may apply separately based on their relationship.

### 2. Living funds and evidence

For tertiary, English-language and other non-compulsory study:
- NZD 20,000 for each year of study, or
- NZD 1,667 per month for study shorter than 1 year.

For compulsory schooling (Years 1–13):
- NZD 17,000 per year, or
- NZD 1,417 per month for shorter study.

Prepaid accommodation may be deducted where accepted. Tuition and outward travel are additional. The applicant must show funds are genuinely available.

Primary evidence may include bank statements with the last 3 months of transactions, fixed-term deposits held at least 3 months, scholarship award letters, eligible education-loan documents or provident funds. INZ may ask for source-of-funds evidence. Explain deposits over NZD 2,000 and multiple unusual deposits with supporting documents. Do not rely on future part-time employment to meet the funds requirement.

Sri Lankan nationals are among nationalities that may be eligible for the Funds Transfer Scheme (FTS), but INZ will advise if FTS is required. Using FTS or opening an account does not guarantee visa approval. Check the applicant's current country-specific INZ instructions and sponsor/financial-undertaking rules.

### 3. Insurance and genuine intentions

- Confirm the student has insurance acceptable to the education provider and covering the period required by the visa. Check the policy, dates, exclusions and any provider-arranged cover.
- Genuine intentions are assessed from the applicant's circumstances and evidence, including course choice, previous study, funding, immigration history and future plans. The applicant must genuinely intend to study and comply with visa conditions.
- English language ability is expressly a key factor in assessing whether a student can meet their visa conditions under instructions strengthened from 27 October 2026. Assess language ability realistically and do not misrepresent it.

### 4. Work rights while studying — 27 October 2026 changes

- Eligible students may be allowed to work up to 25 hours per week during study and full-time during permitted scheduled breaks, only where their individual visa conditions allow it.
- Work rights depend on course type, age, duration and visa conditions. Students aged 16–17 in relevant categories need written permission from a parent/legal guardian and provider; students aged 15 or younger cannot receive student-visa work conditions.
- From 27 October 2026, new student visa applications for master's-by-research programmes no longer receive unlimited in-study work rights. New applicants receive the same work conditions as most other student visa holders. This applies to applications made on or after 27 October 2026.
- Always check the eVisa or visa letter. Do not promise a job, work hours outside the visa conditions or income sufficient to cover tuition/living costs.

### 5. Student visa expiry after course completion

From 27 October 2026, where the visa expiry is linked to course end date, the period after course completion is extended from 1 month to 2 months. The 31 March expiry date remains unchanged for visas with that expiry basis. The student's actual eVisa controls; this is not a guaranteed post-study work visa or general grace period.

### 6. Post-Study Work Visa (PSWV)

Current INZ qualification categories include:
- NZQCF Level 8–10 qualification studied full-time for at least 30 weeks in New Zealand;
- Level 7 bachelor's degree studied full-time for at least 30 weeks in New Zealand;
- eligible Level 7 Graduate Diploma studied full-time in New Zealand for its full duration, if the graduate also holds a bachelor's degree; and
- specified Level 4–7 non-degree qualifications on INZ's current eligible list.

From 16 November 2026, eligible Level 7 Graduate Diploma graduates with a bachelor's degree (New Zealand or overseas) can qualify if they studied the full qualification duration in New Zealand. Cross-crediting or recognition of prior learning does not count for the full-duration test. Eligible Level 8 Postgraduate Diploma graduates may qualify for 1 year; eligible Level 9 Master's graduates may qualify for up to 3 years, subject to at least 30 weeks full-time study in the qualification. Non-degree Level 4–7 qualifications on the eligible list may restrict work to a related occupation.

Check exact NZQCF level, full-time enrolment, weeks studied in New Zealand, cross-credit/RPL, the current eligible list, previous PSWV history, application deadline and required funds. A course title or provider marketing claim is not enough.

### 7. Short-Term Graduate Work Visa — opened 16 November 2026

INZ introduced a 6-month Short-Term Graduate Work Visa for eligible graduates. Published eligibility includes an NZQCF Level 5–7 qualification studied full-time for at least 24 weeks in New Zealand; English-language, foundation and bridging qualifications are excluded, as are qualifications that already make the applicant eligible for a PSWV. Applicants generally must apply within 3 months of student visa expiry, show NZD 5,000 and meet health/character requirements. Applicants must not previously have held a PSWV or Short-Term Graduate Work Visa. Work conditions are employer-specific. Do not market this as a general open work or job-seeker visa.
