# Poland — Student Immigration Source Review
**Review date:** 25 November 2026  
**Status:** Research draft — all dashboard rules remain **Pending human review**.  
**Scope:** Non-EU/EEA applicants, including Sri Lankan nationals; full-time higher education and doctoral study.

## 1. Main route
For a degree programme lasting more than 90 days, the usual sequence is admission to an eligible Polish higher-education institution, application for a national Type D study visa through the Polish consular/VAC route responsible for the applicant, travel to Poland, then (if the stay requires it) an application for a temporary residence permit for study while the applicant is legally in Poland. A short-stay Schengen C visa is not the standard long-term degree route.

The MOS/Office for Foreigners guidance defines the study permit route primarily for full-time first- or second-cycle studies, long-cycle master's studies, doctoral school and specified preparatory courses. Part-time higher education, postgraduate and MBA courses are not automatically covered by this student-study permit; the appropriate residence basis must be checked separately.

## 2. Sri Lankan application jurisdiction
The official Poland-in-India D-study visa page explicitly lists Sri Lanka among the countries handled by the Embassy of Poland in New Delhi. It directs applicants to submit personally at a Visa Application Centre, register study-visa applications through e-Konsulat, and complete the additional VFS form. The current page states study appointment allocation at New Delhi on Monday, Tuesday and Wednesday at 10:00, but this operational schedule can change. Confirm live VFS location, appointment availability, fees and document checklist before every client application. Do not describe Colombo as an assured submission centre unless the live official VFS booking system confirms it.

## 3. Temporary residence permit — eligibility and duration
The official MOS permit page says applicants must be legally in Poland when applying; applications cannot be submitted from abroad. The first permit is normally 15 months, or the academic year/study period plus three months if that is shorter. Certain EU mobility programmes have a two-year first permit; doctoral-school first permits may be 2 years and 6 months. Subsequent study permits may cover the remaining study period plus three months, up to three years (doctoral-school provisions differ). A permit is not automatically renewed.

The MOS page states that from 27 April 2026 study-purpose temporary residence applications are submitted electronically through MOS. The applicant must create a new MOS account, complete the Polish-language application, attach a digital photo and scans of all passport pages, have the university complete and electronically sign its annex, then personally sign electronically and submit. The application is not considered submitted until the official acknowledgement (UPO) is issued. The authority may still summon the applicant for passport presentation, fingerprints and signature.

## 4. Financial evidence
The MOS guidance currently states that, after deducting actual accommodation costs, monthly funds must exceed PLN 1,010 for a student with no dependants, or PLN 823 for the student and each dependant. In addition, the applicant must show return-travel funds; the page lists PLN 2,500 for countries outside its neighbouring-country and specified European categories, which includes Sri Lanka. Tuition fees are separate and must also be covered; proof of semester/year tuition payment is required for fee-paying study. These are statutory permit assessment figures, not a realistic all-in monthly living budget or a fixed visa bank-balance guarantee. Verify the regulation and amounts at the date of application.

## 5. Health insurance and documents
For the study residence permit, provide health insurance under the Polish public healthcare rules or confirmation of an insurer covering medical treatment in Poland. Core evidence includes valid passport, digital photo, university admission/student annex, tuition payment where applicable, insurance, financial means, accommodation cost evidence, and sworn Polish translations where required. The national D visa page states travel medical insurance of at least EUR 30,000 for the intended stay if using that insurance route; confirm the current checklist and policy wording with the competent mission.

## 6. Work during studies
The Office for Foreigners states that a person staying on a Polish national visa issued for full-time studies under Article 60(1)(9), or a study temporary residence permit under Article 144(1), may work without a separate work permit. It also recognises continued work while awaiting the study residence decision in the specific case where the application was made during the qualifying study-visa stay and is formally complete (or deficiencies were corrected on time). Do not generalise this to part-time courses, other visa categories, informal work, or every pending application. Confirm the student's actual visa/permit basis and conditions.

## 7. After graduation
The official MOS graduate Q&A states that an eligible graduate may obtain a one-time temporary residence permit for 9 months to seek employment or prepare to conduct business. The applicant must be legally staying in Poland in connection with the relevant studies/doctoral school and provide the Polish higher-education diploma. The permit is not renewable as a graduate permit. It is not a job offer, work permit or guarantee of employment; the graduate must change to an appropriate residence/work basis before the graduate permit expires.

## 8. Fees and processing
The MOS page lists PLN 340 stamp duty for the study temporary residence permit. A separate residence-card fee applies; the study.gov.pl guidance currently describes a standard PLN 100 card fee and a possible reduced PLN 50 fee for eligible students. The national D-visa fee is subject to the current consular fee table and VFS service charges. The Poland-in-India page says national visa decisions are normally made within 15 days and may extend to 30 days in justified cases. Do not promise processing time.

## Counsellor verification checklist
- Confirm institution is eligible/approved to admit foreign students and exact programme, level, full-time status, campus and duration.
- Confirm the applicant's academic entry requirements, language evidence, tuition, refund terms and genuine study rationale directly with the institution.
- Determine the correct visa type and New Delhi/VAC jurisdiction; verify current e-Konsulat/VFS process, appointment and checklist.
- Verify insurance wording, accommodation costs, funds, tuition and return-travel funds using current rules.
- Explain that part-time/postgraduate/MBA study may not qualify for the Article 144 student permit or the same work exemption.
- Explain that work is not guaranteed; verify the actual visa/permit basis before advising employment.
- Explain the nine-month graduate route is one-time and is not a job guarantee.
- Record source URL, access date, publication/effective date where available, reviewer and decision. Keep all rules Pending human review until approved.
