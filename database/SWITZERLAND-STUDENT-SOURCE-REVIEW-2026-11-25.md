# Switzerland — Student Immigration Source Review
**Review date:** 25 November 2026  
**Status:** Research draft — all dashboard rules remain **Pending human review**.  
**Scope:** Sri Lankan / other non-EU/EFTA applicants seeking study in Switzerland.

## 1. Route and decision authority
For a stay exceeding 90 days, the normal entry visa is a national Visa D, not a short-stay Schengen C visa. The D visa is subject to authorisation by the migration authority of the canton where the student intends to reside. Admission to a school is not itself visa approval. [Swiss FDFA — visa requirements](https://www.eda.admin.ch/en/visa-requirements-for-entry-into-switzerland)

## 2. Sri Lanka submission route and 2026 checklist
The Embassy of Switzerland in Colombo is the national visa submission point for Sri Lankan residents. The Embassy's February 2026 student checklist requires personal attendance by prior appointment; three signed D application forms; four identical photos; passport/previous passport evidence; academic certificates and CV; signed motivation/future-plan/return-intention letter; Swiss admission/registration and tuition-payment evidence; sponsor undertaking and six months of bank movements/balance evidence; sponsor identity and relationship evidence; and language-test evidence. Sinhala/Tamil documents require official translation into English, German, French or Italian. The Embassy may request more documents. [Embassy Colombo — national visa jurisdiction](https://www.schweiz-srilanka.eda.admin.ch/en/where-to-apply-for-a-national-visa) · [2026 Student Visa D checklist](https://www.eda.admin.ch/content/dam/countries/countries-content/sri-lanka/en/2026-visa-checklist/2026_STUDENT_Visarequirements.pdf)

The checklist says the visa fee is free of charge. It estimates approximately three months for the cantonal decision and up to five days for visa processing after authorisation. It instructs arrival registration within 14 days. Treat these as checklist estimates/instructions and reconfirm before every application.

## 3. Permit duration and renewal
The residence permit is canton-administered. Geneva's current page says a course under one year may receive a permit for the course duration; multi-year courses generally receive a one-year permit renewable until completion, and Geneva sets an eight-year maximum for authorised courses. These are Geneva-specific details, not a nationwide promise. [Canton of Geneva — non-EU/EFTA student permit](https://www.ge.ch/en/ask-student-permit/student-permit-nationals-non-eu-country)

## 4. Financial evidence
The Colombo checklist requires a sponsor's written undertaking, six months of bank statements/account movements and bank balance confirmation, sponsor ID, and relationship proof. Federal rules require sufficient means; the amount and acceptable evidence must be confirmed with the relevant canton. No universal nationwide student amount was confirmed in the official sources reviewed. Do not advise CHF 21,000 or CHF 30,000 as a fixed nationwide student visa threshold without written canton-specific confirmation. Tuition, housing, health insurance and daily living costs must be budgeted separately. [SEM — residence without gainful activity](https://www.sem.admin.ch/en/faq-free-movement-of-persons)

## 5. Work during studies
For non-EU/EFTA students at Swiss universities/universities of applied sciences, cantonal authorities may authorise supplementary work no earlier than six months after studies begin. Federal guidance sets a maximum of 15 hours per week outside holidays; the school must confirm compatibility with studies and the employer must submit the application. Obtain written approval before work starts. Some cantonal guidance permits full-time work during university holidays; check the student's own canton. This is not an automatic right for every private language school or vocational course. [SEM — FAQ Working](https://www.sem.admin.ch/en/faq-working) · [Canton Zurich — Students and interns](https://www.zh.ch/en/wirtschaft-arbeit/erwerbstaetigkeit-auslaender/studierende-und-praktikanten.html)

## 6. Post-study job search
Third-country nationals who graduate from a recognised Swiss higher-education institution may have six months after completion to seek employment matching their qualifications. Employment is not automatic: the proposed work must represent an overriding scientific or economic interest and meet permit conditions. Up to 15 hours/week may be authorised during this period. This is not a general route for every private college or language-course graduate. [SEM — FAQ Working](https://www.sem.admin.ch/en/faq-working)

## 7. Arrival, registration and insurance
The Embassy checklist instructs registration with the residents' registration office within 14 days of entry. Federal guidance says people residing in Switzerland for more than three months must take out Swiss health insurance, subject to applicable rules/exemptions. Confirm the commune/canton process, deadline and any exemption directly. [SEM — residence FAQ](https://www.sem.admin.ch/en/faq-free-movement-of-persons)

## 8. Counsellor safeguards / unresolved checks
- Confirm the exact canton and its current student-permit checklist, financial amount, renewal deadline and local registration process.
- Confirm the institution is recognised/accredited and that the programme qualifies for the intended residence route.
- Do not promise part-time employment, full-time holiday work, post-study employment or permanent residence.
- Obtain the current Colombo appointment and checklist instructions before accepting a file; do not rely on old third-country or India route instructions.
- Keep all dashboard entries **Pending human review** until an authorised reviewer verifies source wording, effective dates, canton applicability and the student's exact case.

## 9. Current Embassy / SEM source recheck — 26 November 2026

### National visa and Sri Lankan submission route
- The current Embassy of Switzerland in Sri Lanka national-visa landing page and jurisdiction page were rechecked. The jurisdiction page, published 31 August 2026, expressly says Sri Lanka residents submit national visa applications to the Embassy of Switzerland in Colombo (the Maldives are also covered).
- The current national-visa document page, also published 31 August 2026, says the student checklist PDF is listed as updated 9 June 2026 and that the application is forwarded to the competent cantonal authority. The Embassy can issue the visa only after cantonal approval.
- The actual student PDF is printed 02/2026. It requires personal attendance by prior appointment; three signed D forms; four identical photos; current and previous passport evidence; academic records, CV and employment history; a signed personal statement covering study rationale, future plans and intention to leave after studies; admission/registration confirmation and tuition-payment proof; sponsor undertaking and six months of bank movements/balance evidence; sponsor ID and relationship proof; language-test evidence; and official translations of Sinhala/Tamil/Dhivehi documents into English, German, French or Italian. Two A4 photocopy sets are required. Originals are shown and returned. The Embassy may request further documents. The listed visa processing fee is free of charge.
- The printed checklist states passport validity of at least three months beyond intended departure, at least two blank pages and previous passport evidence (or police report if lost). Apply the checklist literally but reconfirm current version and individual exceptions before filing.

### Processing-time discrepancy
- The current Embassy national-visa web page (published 31 August 2026) gives an approximate total processing time of 8–12 weeks and warns that it may vary considerably by canton and application.
- The student PDF printed 02/2026 instead says approximately three months for the cantonal decision and a further five days for visa issuance after authorisation.
- Retain this as a source discrepancy; do not promise either timeline. Obtain the latest case-specific estimate from the Embassy and competent canton, and do not recommend non-refundable travel before visa issuance.

### Funds, work and post-study route — rechecked
- The Embassy checklist requires a written sponsor undertaking accepting responsibility for living and study costs, six months of bank-account movements and a bank balance letter, sponsor passport/ID, and relationship proof (including parents' birth certificate where parents sponsor). The checklist does not state a universal CHF minimum.
- Federal SEM guidance requires sufficient resources to avoid reliance on social assistance. A single nationwide student amount was not found in the current official sources checked. The exact calculation period, amount, account ownership, sponsor structure and acceptable evidence remain canton-specific; never quote CHF 21,000 or CHF 30,000 as a universal legal threshold.
- For non-EU/EFTA students at a university or university of applied sciences, the canton may authorise supplementary employment no earlier than six months after the course starts, generally up to 15 hours/week outside holidays. The university must confirm compatibility and that work will not delay completion; the employer submits the application. Written authorisation is required before work begins. Holiday rules and other institution categories must be confirmed with the canton.
- Eligible third-country graduates of recognised Swiss higher-education institutions may have up to six months to seek work matching their qualifications. Employment is not automatic; SEM describes the overriding scientific or economic interest test. Do not extend this pathway to every private college or language-course graduate.
- After entry, the printed checklist says registration with the residents' registration office within 14 days. Swiss health insurance is generally required for residence over three months, subject to statutory rules and recognised exemptions; confirm local registration and insurance with the canton/commune.
