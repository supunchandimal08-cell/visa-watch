# Austria Student Residence Permit — Official Source Review (25 November 2026)

## Scope and status
For Sri Lankan / other non-EEA applicants seeking a full-time course in Austria. The standard route for a stay exceeding six months is the Residence Permit – Student (Aufenthaltsbewilligung – Student), not a Schengen short-stay visa. This is a counsellor reference, not a legal decision. Dashboard rules remain Pending human review.

## 1. Course and admission eligibility
- Non-EEA nationals who do not already have another right of residence generally need a Residence Permit – Student.
- Eligible study includes regular degree programmes at Austrian universities, universities of applied sciences, accredited private universities and teacher-education institutions.
- Certain non-degree university programmes may qualify, including programmes of at least 40 ECTS; preparatory programmes for supplementary examinations may also qualify. A language-only course does not automatically qualify.
- Admission is decided by the institution. For regular university degree admission, an A-level/high-school certificate must be equivalent to the Austrian Matura; the applicant may need to show that the certificate also gives access to the same or a similar course in the country where it was obtained. Additional exams and German-language requirements can apply depending on programme.
- Confirm exact institution, programme, language of instruction, admission conditions and legal eligibility before taking a deposit or promising a visa route.

## 2. Sri Lanka application route — important
- The Austrian Embassy New Delhi has consular jurisdiction for Sri Lanka.
- The Embassy's official application instructions state that applications for residence permits for stays exceeding six months must be submitted in person at the Austrian Embassy in New Delhi. Applicants should contact NEW-DELHI-KA@bmeia.gv.at for the residence-permit appointment and current filing instructions.
- Do not confuse this with visa applications up to six months, for which the Embassy uses VFS application centres including Sri Lanka. A visa D for 91–180 days is not the same as a Residence Permit – Student for a longer degree course.
- The Embassy reports high appointment demand; apply early. OeAD general guidance says to allow around three months for the residence-permit process and it can take longer if documents are requested. This is guidance, not a guaranteed decision time.

## 3. 2026 financial means
OeAD's 2026 checklist lists the following monthly living-funds amounts, normally proven for 12 months in advance:
- Under 24 years: €722.58/month = €8,670.96 for 12 months.
- Age 24 or older: €1,308.39/month = €15,700.68 for 12 months.
- Couple: €2,064.12/month; each child additionally €201.88/month.
- The accommodation-cost allowance included in the calculation is €386.43/month. If actual accommodation costs exceed this, additional funds must be shown. Health-insurance costs must also be covered.
Funds must be in an account/savings book in the applicant's name and accessible from Austria. The authority may ask for the origin of funds. Parent/sponsor support may be used with evidence such as the sponsor's six-month bank statements, income evidence and a declaration that funds are available to the student. A family sponsorship letter alone should not be assumed sufficient.
These are living-funds amounts, not tuition fees or a full cost-of-study estimate.

## 4. Health insurance, accommodation and documents
- Health insurance must cover all risks in Austria. OeAD states student self-insurance with the Austrian Health Insurance Fund may be available after arrival/enrolment; its 2026 monthly premium is €78.84, subject to eligibility. Private insurance must meet Austrian authority standards; ordinary travel insurance alone is not sufficient.
- OeAD checklist asks for accommodation evidence for at least three months and considers actual accommodation costs in the financial calculation.
- Common first-application documents include signed application, valid passport and copies of all pages, ICAO-format photo, admission letter, proof of funds and source of funds, accommodation information, health-insurance evidence, and a police clearance certificate from the country of residence (and sometimes country of origin) issued within three months, legalised and translated as required.
- Foreign documents may need legalisation and certified German translation. English documents may be accepted in some cases, but confirm with the authority.
- OeAD lists an application fee of €218. Fees are not refundable if refused or withdrawn. Recheck the current fee and any additional document/legalisation charges.

## 5. Work during studies
- Third-country students need an employment permit (Beschäftigungsbewilligung), including for marginal employment.
- The employer must apply to the Austrian Public Employment Service (AMS) before work starts.
- A permit may be issued for work up to 20 hours per week without a labour-market test. More than 20 hours is not automatically allowed; it can require a labour-market test.
- Work must not jeopardise study as the primary purpose of residence. Do not promise employment, hours or earnings, and do not advise students to start work before the permit is granted.

## 6. Arrival, residence card and renewal
- Register the place of residence (Meldezettel) within three working days of arrival, subject to the stated accommodation exceptions.
- Collect the Residence Permit – Student from the competent Austrian residence authority; OeAD indicates the student must show residence registration and final admission/enrolment, and insurance evidence where relevant.
- The permit is normally issued for 12 months, unless a shorter period is requested or passport validity is shorter.
- Apply for renewal before expiry, at the earliest three months before expiry. OeAD lists proof of continuing enrolment and academic success of at least 8 semester hours or 16 ECTS per academic year; doctoral students may submit confirmation of academic progress from their supervisor.
- Change of study programme must be reported and may affect renewal. Check the individual case and current authority rules.

## 7. After graduation
A graduate who held a Residence Permit – Student may apply for a one-time 12-month extension to seek employment or establish a business. This is not a job guarantee. A later switch to a Red-White-Red Card or another residence title requires meeting that route's requirements.

## Counsellor checklist
1. Confirm age (under 24 vs 24+), nationality and current country of residence.
2. Verify institution accreditation/recognition, course type, ECTS, language, admission and any entrance/supplementary exams.
3. For Sri Lankan residents applying for a course longer than six months, direct them to contact the Austrian Embassy New Delhi for an in-person residence-permit appointment; do not tell them to submit the long-term permit application at a local VFS centre.
4. Calculate the 12-month funds using the correct age band, then add accommodation costs above €386.43/month, insurance, tuition, travel and setup costs.
5. Verify source-of-funds documents and sponsor evidence; assess whether funds are accessible in Austria.
6. Explain employer-applied AMS work permit and 20-hour limit; no job or income guarantee.
7. Explain Meldezettel within three working days, collection of the permit and renewal academic-progress conditions.
8. Keep the rule Pending human review until the authorised company reviewer approves it.
