UK · AUSTRALIA · NEW ZEALAND

Provider and course eligibility — verify the exact enrolment

Do not approve a destination or course based only on an agent brochure, university logo, partner claim or the fact that an institution is well known. Verify the institution, campus, exact course, delivery arrangement and the visa-specific enrolment document against the responsible official register.

United Kingdom — licensed Student sponsor + eligible course + CAS.

Search the current GOV.UK Register of licensed sponsors: Students. Match the legal institution name, sponsor category and rating; check the register again close to application because a licence can change. A sponsor being listed is not by itself enough: verify the exact course meets the Student route requirements, the qualification and level rules, study location and any relevant ATAS requirement. Obtain the CAS issued for that applicant and course; check the CAS details, fees paid/accommodation, course dates, place of study and sponsor licence status. GOV.UK says the visa application must be made within 6 months of receiving the CAS. Do not treat a conditional offer, agent confirmation or deposit receipt as a CAS.

Stop rule: if the institution is absent from the live register, the course or delivery site cannot be reconciled with the CAS, or the CAS details are incorrect/withdrawn, stop and refer to the institution and compliance reviewer before filing.

Australia — CRICOS provider + exact CRICOS course + valid CoE.

Search the Australian Government CRICOS register for both the provider and the exact course. Match the CRICOS provider code, course CRICOS code, campus/location, course duration, delivery mode and current registration dates. CRICOS registration is the official register for courses/providers permitted to enrol overseas students on student visas. Then obtain and check the Confirmation of Enrolment (CoE) for every intended course. Home Affairs requires a valid CoE for the intended full-time course(s) at application; a cancelled or completed CoE is no longer valid, and packaged courses need all relevant CoE codes included. Recheck CoE validity when the Department decides the application. Do not assume a provider's CRICOS registration covers every course or campus.

Stop rule: if the provider or exact course/campus is not currently CRICOS registered, the course code differs from the offer, or the CoE is cancelled/invalid, do not proceed on the basis of an agent or provider marketing page.

New Zealand — approved provider + Code signatory + offer of place.

Check the education provider's status and whether it is a signatory to the Education (Pastoral Care of Tertiary and International Learners) Code of Practice 2021 using the official NZQA information/register. Immigration New Zealand says international students cannot study at an institution that is not a Code signatory. Check the exact campus, qualification/programme and offer of place; the provider must assess that the course is appropriate to the student's expectations and that the student has the English ability and academic capability to pass. Confirm tuition, course dates, full-time/part-time status, insurance arrangements and the current visa requirements. Provider Code signatory status is a required provider check, not a promise of visa approval.

Stop rule: if the provider's Code status, programme approval/quality status, campus or offer details cannot be confirmed, pause the recommendation and obtain written clarification from the provider/NZQA or escalate to compliance.

Evidence to save for every destination: date/time checked; official register URL and saved evidence; legal provider name; provider ID/code; campus; exact course title/code; qualification/level; delivery mode; start/end dates; enrolment document (CAS/CoE/offer); reviewer and decision. Recheck before application because register status and course details can change.

Post-study stay — 2026 federal guidance.

After successfully completing a degree in Germany, a third-country graduate may apply for a residence permit for up to 18 months to seek qualified employment. During that period, any type of work is permitted while seeking qualified employment. The applicant must apply before the study permit expires and show graduation, health insurance and secured livelihood. This is a separate post-study permit, not an automatic extension or a job guarantee. Official federal guidance: Prospects after graduation.

UNITED KINGDOM

Student visa — financial evidence, work conditions and upcoming threshold change

Important date control (checked 13 November 2026): do not use one maintenance figure for every application date. The current Immigration Rules amounts remain £1,529 per month in London and £1,171 outside London (up to 9 months) for applications made before 30 November 2026. HC 584 changes these to £1,570 and £1,203 respectively for applications made on or after 30 November 2026. The amendment has a specific commencement and transitional rule; record the actual application date and apply the correct version. The GOV.UK summary page may lag the formal rule amendment, so check Appendix Student and HC 584 at lodgement.

1. Calculate course fees + maintenance separately.

For most applicants, include unpaid course fees for one academic year (up to 9 months) as stated on the CAS, plus living costs based on the main place of study: London or outside London. If the course period includes part of a month, round up to the next full month; maintenance is capped at 9 months. For applications before 30 November 2026, the 9-month maximum is £13,761 in London or £10,539 outside London. For applications made on or after 30 November 2026, it becomes £14,130 in London or £10,827 outside London. If the sponsor confirms tuition already paid on the CAS, or a valid receipt is provided, account for that payment correctly. A sponsor-arranged accommodation deposit may be offset only up to £1,529 before 30 November 2026, increasing to £1,570 from that date. Do not calculate London status from the student's home address: it depends on the study location.

2. Check the 28-day holding and 31-day evidence window.

Where bank funds are required, the applicant normally needs to hold the required amount for 28 consecutive days; the end of that period must be within 31 days before the application date. Check every statement date and daily balance, account holder, bank regulation, immediate access and currency conversion. A sudden deposit or balance dip can create a refusal risk. A qualifying student loan or official financial sponsorship is assessed under its own evidence rules.

3. Parent / family funds — document the relationship and access.

GOV.UK permits reliance on a parent's money with the required parent consent letter and relationship evidence, provided the account and funds meet Appendix Finance. Do not treat a general family support promise, sponsor letter alone or inaccessible asset as available cash. Overdrafts, cryptocurrency, shares, pensions and non-regulated or non-electronic accounts are not acceptable evidence under the published financial-evidence guidance. Sri Lanka is not on the published differential-evidence nationality list, so a Sri Lankan applicant should normally prepare financial evidence unless another specific exemption applies. UKVI may still request evidence from nationals covered by differential arrangements.

4. Work rights depend on sponsor type and course level — not just the visa label.

Under the current caseworker guidance and Appendix Student ST 26, an eligible full-time degree-level or above student at a qualifying Higher Education Provider with a track record of compliance can generally work up to 20 hours per week during term-time and full-time during official vacations. A qualifying full-time below-degree student at such a provider may have a 10-hour term-time limit. Students sponsored by other provider categories, and students on part-time courses, may have no employment permission except an authorised work placement where the rules allow it. Check the exact work condition printed on the eVisa/decision and the sponsor/course category before advising.

Students must not be self-employed or engage in business activity, fill a full-time permanent vacancy (subject to narrow exceptions), or work as a professional sportsperson/entertainer. Do not market part-time work as guaranteed income or as a way to meet the initial financial requirement.

5. Dependant and family rules need a separate eligibility check.

Do not assume a student can bring a spouse or children. Dependant eligibility is restricted and depends on course type, sponsorship and application circumstances. Where a dependant is eligible and needs to show funds, the current separate maintenance figure is £845 per month in London or £680 outside London, for up to 9 months (maximum £7,605 or £6,120 per dependant). These dependant figures are separate from the student's maintenance and are not changed by HC 584's APP ST8–ST10 uplift. Verify the current Appendix Student rules and eligibility for each family member before quoting a total.

6. Evidence and reviewer stop-rule.

Save the CAS, exact course and sponsor register check, study location, course fee balance, financial calculation, bank evidence dates, source of funds, parent consent/relationship evidence if used, English-language method, ATAS/TB requirements if applicable, work-condition category and application date. Stop and escalate if the CAS, sponsor status, course level, bank history or source of funds cannot be reconciled. Never promise visa approval, work or a Graduate route outcome.

UAE / DUBAI

Student residence pathway — sponsor, institution and work checks

Core distinction: student residence is an immigration status for study. It is not itself a general employment permit, a job offer or a guarantee of part-time work. GDRFA describes the student residence permit as a residence permit without a requirement to work; the educational institution acts as host and guarantor for that route.

Short-term study/training visit visa is a different route — do not call it student residence.

GDRFA Dubai separately lists a single-entry visit visa for study, training or rehabilitation for 30, 60 or 180 days, with a letter from the licensed educational host and a passport valid for at least six months. The displayed visit-visa fees and security deposit are separate from the student residence permit fees and may vary by case. This short-stay route must not be presented as a long-term residence visa or as a substitute for the institution-sponsored student residence route. Confirm the purpose, duration, sponsor/host, extension possibility and current charges directly in the live GDRFA service before advising.

1. Confirm the correct sponsor route — do not assume every applicant is institution-sponsored.

The UAE Government states that a student may stay under the sponsorship of a UAE-resident parent or the accredited university/college. For Dubai international students, Visit Dubai says the institution manages the student-visa process through GDRFA. Record the applicant's current location/status, emirate, age, sex where relevant to parental sponsorship rules, and proposed sponsor. Get written confirmation from the institution or relevant authority of who will act as sponsor/host before quoting a visa package. Do not assume a Sri Lankan parent living outside the UAE can sponsor under the UAE-resident-parent route.

2. Verify the institution's legal licence and education-authority approval.

GDRFA says the university, college, institute or research centre must be licensed to conduct the activity in the UAE and must have approval from the Ministry of Education or the competent education authority in the relevant emirate. Verify the legal entity, campus, exact programme and authority responsible for that emirate. A recruitment-agent agreement, trade name, marketing website or admission offer alone does not prove student-residence sponsorship eligibility. Ask the institution's visa/compliance team for written confirmation that it can host/guarantee this student for this exact programme and campus.

Stop rule: pause if the legal provider, campus, programme approval, sponsor/host or visa-processing authority cannot be independently reconciled.

3. Build the applicant-specific document checklist.

GDRFA's Dubai student residence service lists a recent white-background photo, passport copy, approved medical examination, Emirates ID application receipt and health insurance. The UAE Government also says a certificate from the university/institute specifying the study duration is required. Visit Dubai's guidance lists a university admission/study certificate and student undertaking, alongside passport and photo; other documents may apply depending on the applicant's current UAE status. For a first-time applicant outside the UAE, obtain the institution's current written checklist and confirm passport validity, entry-permit steps, medical/Emirates ID sequence, insurance and any current-sponsor cancellation requirements. Do not present this as a universal exhaustive list.

Financial evidence — do not invent a fixed UAE student minimum.

The published GDRFA Dubai student-residence service checklist does not state one universal bank-balance or monthly living-funds threshold. The UAE Government student-visa overview also describes the sponsor route and enrolment certificate but does not publish a single financial amount. Therefore, do not enter an assumed AED amount into the visa-evidence calculator or tell a client that a fixed balance is a nationwide legal requirement. Ask the exact university/college visa office and the relevant emirate authority for the applicant-specific written financial-document checklist, including any tuition-payment, sponsor, deposit or maintenance evidence they require. Record the authority, source URL and date checked. This does not remove the separate need to budget realistically for tuition, housing, insurance and living costs.

4. Quote fees only from the live application route.

The GDRFA Dubai student service page currently displays AED 200 residence-permit fee, AED 10 Knowledge Dirham, AED 10 Innovation Dirham, AED 500 inside-country fee (where applicable) and AED 20 delivery. It notes an additional AED 100 per year where residency exceeds two years. These are displayed service fee components, not a universal all-in student budget and not a guaranteed all-in student visa cost: institution administration, entry permit, medical, Emirates ID, insurance, typing/AMER and other charges may be separate or case-dependent. Confirm the live fee breakdown with the institution/GDRFA before issuing a quotation.

5. Keep Dubai and federal expiry/grace-period statements separate.

The GDRFA Dubai student-permit service page states a 60-day grace period after expiry or cancellation for the Dubai service described (the page showed a website update date of 12 November 2026 when checked). Federal ICP pages describe a separate 180-day grace period for students after completing their studies; the ICP renewal service also lists 60 days for residence permits issued with a guarantor or host. Do not assume every student automatically qualifies for 180 days: the study-completion status, sponsor/host category, emirate and permit record matter. These are distinct authority/category statements, not one blanket UAE rule. Before advising, obtain confirmation from the responsible authority for the student's exact permit and record the source and date checked.

7. Do not market Golden Residence as the normal student visa.

GDRFA lists a separate 10-year Golden Residence route for qualifying top students and graduates. The published criteria include at least 95% for eligible UAE high-school graduates, and specified GPA, graduation-recency, university-ranking and Ministry/ICP approval conditions for eligible university graduates. These are exceptional merit-based categories, not an automatic benefit of ordinary admission or student residence. Check the exact category, qualification authentication, ranking list and nomination/approval with GDRFA/ICP before mentioning eligibility; never promise it based only on A/L passes or an offer letter.

6. Explain work as a separate approval — never promise “part-time work” from student residence alone.

The UAE Government states that working without a valid work permit is illegal and that the employer must obtain the relevant permit before employment starts. MoHRE lists a Student Training and Employment Permit for a student already in the UAE who is at least 15 years old; the permit is issued to the establishment and is listed as valid for three months. This is not an automatic entitlement attached to a student residence visa and is not a guarantee that a job will be available. For a student aged 15–18, juvenile-work protections and additional requirements apply. For student training, the official guidance lists a valid UAE residence visa for expatriate students, Emirates ID evidence of student status, parent/legal-guardian consent for ages 15–18, medical fitness evidence and (for training) a no-objection certificate from the educational institute. The employer must use the applicable MoHRE or relevant free-zone authority process. Check the student's age, current residence status, employer jurisdiction, contract, permit type, institution NOC and current rules before advising. Do not quote a universal student work-hour allowance or count expected earnings as visa financial evidence.

AUSTRALIA

Student visa subclass 500 — financial evidence, work limits and rule checks

Financial capacity — first 12 months.

Home Affairs currently lists living costs of AUD 29,710 for the student, AUD 10,394 for an accompanying partner and AUD 4,449 for each accompanying child. Only include partner/child amounts where the family member is permitted to be included under the current exemption or transitional rules (most new applicants cannot include family under the changes effective 2 October 2026). Where inclusion is permitted, calculate the first 12 months of course fees (or the full fee if the course is shorter), travel costs, and school costs for any school-age child. Home Affairs currently lists at least AUD 13,502 per year for each school-age child (pro rata for a shorter stay), unless a specific government-school fee-waiver exception applies; actual school fees vary by state, territory and school. For an applicant applying from Sri Lanka, Home Affairs' travel-cost guide is AUD 2,000 for an applicant applying from outside Australia (not a fixed airline quote). Deduct tuition already paid only when supported by a receipt or Confirmation of Enrolment. For a stay shorter than 12 months, calculate the relevant annual costs pro rata as directed by Home Affairs. These visa minimums are not a realistic full living-cost budget for every Australian city.

Home Affairs may require evidence of financial capacity through the applicant's ImmiAccount document checklist. If the checklist requires it, attach the evidence when lodging; omission can result in refusal without a further request. Home Affairs may also request it during processing even if the checklist did not initially show it. Evidence examples include deposits with a financial institution, government or financial-institution loans, and scholarships or similar financial support. The applicant must also demonstrate genuine access to the money and be ready to explain its source.

An alternative is evidence that the applicant's parent(s) or partner had personal annual income of at least AUD 87,856 in the 12 months before application; where family members accompany the student, the threshold is AUD 102,500. Home Affairs requires official government income documents (for example, a tax assessment) issued within the previous 12 months; bank statements or direct employer evidence are not accepted for this income alternative. Check the exact applicant circumstances and current instrument before using this option. Do not add this income route to the funds route or count the same money twice.

Work cap while study is in session: the Department of Home Affairs currently states that most Student visa holders may work up to 48 hours per fortnight while their course is in session. The fortnight is a rolling 14-day period starting on a Monday; calculate the total across all jobs, not separately for each employer.

Before course commencement.

Student visa holders generally cannot work before their course starts, unless they held another visa with work permission at the time they applied. Confirm the actual visa conditions and grant date.

During scheduled course breaks.

Students may generally work unlimited hours when the course is not in session. A scheduled break must be confirmed against the provider's official academic calendar; do not assume every gap between classes is a holiday.

Exceptions and placements.

Students undertaking a masters by research or doctoral degree may have no work-hour limit after the relevant course has commenced. A work placement can be excluded from the cap only when it is a mandatory, registered component of the CRICOS course and within the required period. Do not apply this exception to optional placements or ordinary paid work experience.

Individual conditions override a generic summary.

Check the visa grant notice and VEVO for the applicant's actual conditions (including condition 8105), and verify the current legislative instrument before advising. A work-hour limit is not a job offer or guarantee of employment.

2026 application-location and family-member changes — effective 2 October 2026

Onshore applications: from 2 October 2026, most Student visa applications must be lodged outside Australia. Certain temporary visa holders (including Visitor 600, Temporary Graduate 485, Working Holiday 417/462 and several other listed subclasses) cannot apply onshore; Home Affairs says there are no exemptions for holders of the listed visas. Current Student visa holders also generally need to apply offshore for a further Student visa unless a specific exemption applies (for example, up to 12 additional months to complete the same main course with the same provider, PhD study, specified school study, eligible course progression, DFAT/Defence sponsorship or provider default). A Student visa dependant cannot simply switch to primary-applicant status onshore. Use the live Home Affairs eligibility list for the exact visa subclass and exemption before advising.

Grant-location rule: if the applicant was required to lodge offshore under these rules, they must also be outside Australia when the Student visa is granted. Returning to Australia on another valid visa while the application is pending does not remove that requirement; no bridging visa is linked to an offshore application of this kind. An onshore application lodged when the applicant is required to be offshore may be invalid. Do not advise a client to travel or lodge until the location rule is checked.

Family members: from 2 October 2026, most new Student visa applicants cannot include a partner or dependent child unless an exemption applies. Home Affairs lists exemptions including PhD students, DFAT/Defence-sponsored students, recipients of a fully funded foreign-government scholarship and eligible Pacific/ASEAN nationals. Sri Lanka is not on the listed Pacific/ASEAN nationality categories, so a Sri Lankan applicant should not assume they can include dependants; assess any other exemption carefully. Current Student visa holders cannot add family as later “subsequent entrants” after grant. Narrow transitional cases for family already included on a current visa (such as certain course-completion or provider-default cases) need a separate check. Eligible family members must apply at the same time and same location as the primary applicant.

Transition: applications lodged before 2 October 2026 continue to be assessed under the rules in force when lodged, including family members already included in those applications.

Commencement-date discrepancy: the Federal Register metadata page displays “Effective 1 October 2026”, while section 2 of the as-made instrument says the whole instrument commences the day after registration. As it was registered on 1 October, the operative commencement clause gives 2 October 2026. Record the registration date and operative commencement date separately; apply the transitional provision to applications made on or after commencement.

CANADA

Study permit — financial evidence and student work

Financial evidence: IRCC requires proof of enough money, without working in Canada, for tuition, living expenses for the applicant and accompanying family members, and transportation to and from Canada. Applicants must show first-year funds and explain how they will pay for the full duration of longer programmes. IRCC assesses both the amount and the source of funds.

Living-cost amount for applications received on or after 1 September 2026.

For applicants outside Quebec, the current IRCC table is:

  • 1 person: CAD 23,448
  • 2 people: CAD 29,192
  • 3 people: CAD 35,888
  • 4 people: CAD 43,572
  • 5 people: CAD 49,419
  • 6 people: CAD 55,736
  • 7 people: CAD 62,054
  • Each additional family member: CAD 6,318

Count the applicant and accompanying family members. These amounts exclude tuition and transportation. Quebec has separate provincial financial requirements; do not apply this table to Quebec applicants. Use the table that matches the actual application-received date.

Evidence and source of funds.

IRCC examples include tuition/housing payment receipts (with separate living/travel funds), education loans, scholarships, Canadian bank account evidence, GICs, foreign or Canadian bank statements for the past 6 months, pension documents, rental income, and a support letter with the supporter's occupation, relationship, dependants and amount of support. Support letters should be accompanied by relationship evidence and the supporter's ID or business registration. Bank statements should explain the source of deposits/income. These are examples, not an exhaustive checklist; check the applicant's local visa-office instructions. If foreign-exchange controls apply, evidence may be needed that funds can be exported.

PAL / TAL — 2026 study-permit cap and exemptions.

Most applicants to study in a Canadian province or territory need a Provincial Attestation Letter (PAL) or Territorial Attestation Letter (TAL). The province/territory issues it, usually through the DLI. Submit it with the study permit application; do not plan to submit it later. IRCC says a PAL/TAL for the 2026 cap year is generally valid until 31 December 2026 unless it has an earlier expiry date. A PAL/TAL issued in a previous cap year cannot be used for a 2026 application.

From 1 January 2026, applicants entering a degree-granting master's or doctoral programme at a public DLI are exempt from PAL/TAL. The exemption is not a blanket exemption for all graduate certificates, private colleges, or every programme described as postgraduate. Check that the institution is on IRCC's public-DLI graduate-program list and that the programme is degree-granting. Primary and secondary students and certain other listed categories are also exempt.

Quebec: a PAL/TAL is not the whole provincial requirement. Most students who do not need a PAL/TAL still need a valid Quebec Acceptance Certificate (CAQ), unless a specific Quebec exemption applies. Check the CAQ validity and wording with the Quebec authority and DLI.

Changes after a PAL/TAL: a new PAL/TAL is generally needed when changing schools or levels of study, with stated exceptions. An extension at the same DLI and same level is exempt; restoring student status requires a new PAL/TAL. A move from post-secondary study to a degree-granting master's/doctoral programme at a public DLI is also treated as an exemption. Check IRCC's current scenario table before reusing a letter.

Doctoral applicants — check the current faster-processing pathway.

IRCC currently states that doctoral students and their accompanying family members may have their study-permit applications processed in 2 weeks. Treat this as an IRCC processing initiative, not a guaranteed decision time or approval. Confirm the applicant is genuinely admitted to an eligible doctoral programme, has a complete application and meets all ordinary study-permit requirements. Check the live IRCC graduate-student instructions and processing information immediately before setting client expectations; do not apply this timeline to master’s, college or other applicants.

Sri Lanka application checklist — check regional instructions.

IRCC's Colombo visa-office checklist (IMM 5864 E, February 2026) asks applicants residing in Sri Lanka to provide evidence of accumulated funds with a history of at least 6 months: bank book/statements showing the financial history and balance, plus a written explanation of the source of funds. It says other assets (such as investments, bonds, real estate, rental income, pension or business transactions) may be included but must not replace the requested bank evidence. Where parents provide support, the checklist asks for original employment letters for each parent with employer contact details, date hired, role, current salary, bonuses and other income, or parents' income-tax documents for the past two years. If a parent owns a business, it asks for notarised business registration/licence showing shareholding, business tax assessments for the past two years, and audited financial statements/capital verification for the most recent fiscal year. If someone other than a parent provides support, include the same financial/employment information for that supporter, a written explanation why that person is supporting the applicant, and relationship evidence where applicable. The checklist also requests a one-page study plan addressing the programme choice, educational goal, why a similar programme is not being pursued in the country of residence/citizenship, and how the programme may improve employment opportunities there; explain education or career gaps where relevant. This is a local checklist: confirm the latest version and applicability to the applicant's place of residence before filing.

Off-campus work during regular study periods.

Eligible students may work up to 24 hours per week during regular academic sessions. The student must meet IRCC eligibility and study-permit conditions, including eligible DLI/full-time enrolment requirements and having started the programme. Students enrolled only in ESL/FSL, general-interest courses or prerequisite courses are not eligible for off-campus work under these rules. More than 24 hours during regular sessions can breach permit conditions.

Scheduled breaks.

Eligible students may work unlimited hours during a scheduled break only if it is scheduled by the school and lasts at least 7 consecutive days. IRCC states a maximum of 180 days of unlimited off-campus work during a calendar year; special limits apply to back-to-back breaks longer than 150 days. A statutory holiday or long weekend by itself is not a scheduled break. Confirm the official DLI calendar and permit conditions.

Co-op / internship placements.

Work placements have separate eligibility rules. IRCC says post-secondary students no longer need a co-op work permit for placements from 1 April 2026, subject to the applicable eligibility framework. Confirm the placement is a required component of the programme and review current IRCC instructions before advising.

Post-Graduation Work Permit (PGWP) — check before recommending a Canadian course.

PGWP is not automatic after graduation. The applicant must complete an eligible programme at a PGWP-eligible DLI. IRCC's general criteria currently include a programme at least 8 months long (900 hours for Quebec programmes), full-time status in each semester (part-time may be allowed in the final semester), and applying within 180 days after confirmation of completion; the study permit must have been valid at some point during the 180 days after completion. Check exceptions and the applicant's exact study history.

Language and field-of-study rules: bachelor's, master's and doctoral graduates need CLB 7 / NCLC 7 in all four language skills and have no field-of-study restriction. Other university graduates also need CLB 7 / NCLC 7; if their study-permit application was made on or after 1 November 2024, their programme must be in an eligible field. College, polytechnic and other non-university graduates need CLB 5 / NCLC 5 in all four skills; the same 1 November 2024 study-permit application-date test applies to the eligible-field requirement. Check the current eligible-field list and exact programme/CIP code before giving advice.

High-risk programme checks: English/French as a second language, general-interest/self-improvement and non-credit programmes do not qualify. Many public-private curriculum-licensing partnership programmes are not PGWP-eligible. A DLI's presence on the DLI list alone does not prove that the particular programme qualifies. Distance-learning rules, school transfers and authorised leave can also affect eligibility and permit length.

Length: never promise a three-year PGWP. The permit length depends on programme level, length and applicable IRCC rules; verify the specific case using the official IRCC guidance before quoting any expected duration.

Study-permit refusal-risk review — evidence, not a prediction.

Before submission, reconcile the applicant's study plan with previous education and work history, explain course choice and career relevance, demonstrate credible funds from traceable sources without relying on future Canadian work, confirm the DLI/programme/LOA and PAL/TAL or exemption (and CAQ where relevant), and check identity, travel/immigration history, biometrics, medical and other document requirements for the applicant. Explain gaps, changes of direction, dependants and unusual deposits with evidence. No checklist can predict or guarantee approval; respond to the actual refusal reasons if a previous application was refused.

2026 CIP code verification — do not rely on course title alone.

For non-degree programmes where the field-of-study rule applies, obtain the programme's exact 6-digit CIP 2021 code in writing from the DLI and independently match it to IRCC's current eligible list. A marketing title such as “IT”, “business”, “healthcare” or “management” is not enough to establish the CIP classification. IRCC states that a CIP code must be eligible when the applicant submits the study permit application or the PGWP application; the application-date rules and any grandfathering must be checked for that individual. IRCC has announced that the PGWP eligible-field list is frozen for 2026, meaning no eligible fields will be added or removed during 2026. Save the dated official list and the institution's written CIP confirmation to the client file.

Course-selection stop rule: if the DLI cannot confirm the exact CIP code, the code does not match the programme content, or the code is not eligible for the applicant's relevant application date, do not market the course as PGWP-eligible. Escalate for compliance review before accepting a client deposit or issuing a recommendation.

PGWP duration — never promise a fixed number of years.

The minimum programme length to meet the general eligibility threshold is 8 months (900 hours for Quebec programmes), but the PGWP validity period is a separate calculation. It depends on the programme credential, official programme length, eligible study time in Canada and the current IRCC rules. Online study outside Canada, school transfers and certain study periods may reduce or affect the permit. Do not quote “3 years” based only on a college brochure; check the current IRCC duration guidance for the exact credential and study history.

UNITED STATES

F-1 / M-1 student visa — school, funds, work and practical training

Choose the correct category first. The U.S. Department of State says F visas are generally for academic study (including university/college and language-training programmes), while M visas are for vocational or other recognised non-academic study. A visitor B visa is not a substitute for an F/M visa for a course leading to a U.S. degree or certificate. Confirm the exact course and visa category with the SEVP-approved school before taking payment or filing.

1. Verify the school and Form I-20.

Start with a school approved by the Student and Exchange Visitor Program (SEVP). After acceptance, the school registers the student in SEVIS and issues Form I-20. Match the student's legal name, SEVIS ID, programme, education level, start date, campus and estimated costs. The student and school official must sign the I-20. An offer letter alone is not the visa document; do not proceed if the school cannot issue a valid I-20 for the intended course.

2. Financial evidence — use the I-20 and applicant's actual funding plan.

The U.S. Department of State says a consular officer may request evidence showing how the applicant will pay educational, living and travel costs. Use the current I-20 cost estimate and the school's written tuition/living budget, then reconcile it with genuine, accessible funds, scholarships or qualifying sponsor support. The State Department page does not set one universal national student bank-balance amount. Do not copy another university's budget or count hoped-for part-time earnings as the primary funding plan.

3. Fees and application sequence.

After receiving the I-20, check the SEVIS I-901 fee and pay it for the matching SEVIS ID before the visa interview where required. ICE currently lists the F/M student I-901 fee as USD 350. The State Department currently lists the nonimmigrant F/M visa application fee as USD 185; these are separate fees and are non-refundable where stated. Complete DS-160, follow the U.S. Embassy Colombo's live appointment and document instructions, and bring the I-20, DS-160 confirmation, fee evidence, passport and relevant academic and financial records. Recheck both fees and local instructions immediately before payment because fees and procedures can change.

4. Interview and decision — no approval guarantee.

The consular officer assesses eligibility and may request evidence of academic preparation, intent to depart after the authorised study, and ability to pay all education, living and travel costs. A visa does not guarantee admission at the U.S. border; CBP makes the admission decision. New F/M students may generally be issued a visa up to 365 days before the course begins but cannot enter more than 30 days before the programme start date. Do not promise approval, a decision date or a particular interview outcome.

5. Work during study — strict F-1 conditions.

Eligible F-1 students may work in qualifying on-campus employment up to 20 hours per week while school is in session, and full-time during authorised school breaks. Off-campus work is not a general student entitlement: it requires a specific regulatory basis and, where required, prior USCIS authorisation. Do not tell a student they can work freely off campus or use part-time work to meet initial funding requirements.

6. CPT / OPT are training authorisations, not automatic jobs.

Curricular Practical Training (CPT) must be an integral part of the established curriculum and authorised by the Designated School Official (DSO) in SEVIS before work begins. Optional Practical Training (OPT) must relate directly to the student's major field and requires the required DSO recommendation and USCIS employment authorisation. Standard OPT may be up to 12 months; eligible STEM graduates may qualify for a 24-month extension if all regulatory and employer conditions are met. One year or more of full-time CPT can make a student ineligible for post-completion OPT. Verify the student's programme, timing, SEVIS record and EAD before any work begins.

7. Completion and status maintenance.

The State Department describes a 60-day period after the programme end date (including authorised practical training) for an F student to prepare to depart, unless the student takes an eligible next step such as a new programme or authorised practical training. This is not a general job-search visa. The student must maintain full-time study and F-1 status, follow DSO instructions and avoid unauthorised employment.

JAPAN

Student residence status — Certificate of Eligibility, funds and part-time work

Correct route: Japan's “Student” status of residence covers study at eligible universities, junior colleges, colleges of technology, specialised training colleges and Japanese-language schools. The authorised period of stay is individually determined and can be up to 4 years and 3 months; do not promise every student a fixed visa duration.

1. Confirm the school and Certificate of Eligibility (COE).

The accepting school commonly acts as the student's proxy to apply for a COE at the regional immigration services bureau in Japan. Match the legal school, course, campus, study period and applicant details on the COE. Once issued, the applicant applies for the student visa at the Japanese Embassy/Consulate responsible for their place of residence. A COE supports the visa application but does not guarantee visa issuance. Obtain the current Sri Lanka-specific checklist from the Embassy before filing.

2. Financial evidence — no universal fixed monthly amount in this guidance.

Japan's official Study in Japan guidance says applicants may be asked to prove they can pay expenses during their stay. Examples include the student's or guarantor's savings-balance certificate, income certificate covering the past year and documents showing the source of funds. Ask the school for its current COE screening checklist and budget; reconcile tuition, accommodation, living and travel costs with genuine accessible funds. Do not invent a nationwide JPY bank-balance threshold or rely on future part-time income to fund the entire course.

3. Part-time work requires advance permission.

The Student status itself does not permit work. The student must obtain “Permission to Engage in Activity Other Than That Permitted under the Status of Residence” from immigration. With the normal blanket permission, eligible students may work up to 28 hours per week, and up to 8 hours per day during long school holidays. Prohibited adult-entertainment-related work remains excluded. Permission is tied to the student's enrolment and conditions; after graduation, a former student who is no longer enrolled cannot continue relying on the student part-time permission.

4. Attendance and study purpose matter.

The primary purpose must remain study. Immigration guidance warns that a student who stops attending and works instead may face residence-status revocation. Do not market Japan as a work-first route, promise employment, or tell a client that part-time earnings will reliably cover tuition and living costs.

5. Graduation and job hunting are separate procedures.

Graduates seeking employment may need to change to an appropriate “Designated Activities” status under the applicable job-hunting route and meet its school recommendation and other conditions. A student status or unused student permission is not an automatic post-study work visa. Confirm eligibility with the school's international office and Immigration Services Agency before the current status expires.

SOUTH KOREA

D-2 degree study / D-4 general training — admission, funds and part-time work

Choose the correct status. D-2 is the study status for degree-level study and related recognised programmes; D-4 is for general training such as qualifying Korean-language study. Do not describe a language-school D-4 route as a university degree visa. Confirm the exact visa code, programme, institution and duration with the university and the Korean Embassy responsible for Sri Lanka.

1. Sri Lanka D-2 checklist — use the Embassy's dated list.

The Embassy of the Republic of Korea in Sri Lanka published its D-2 (Bachelor/Master/Doctor) checklist on 25 February 2026. It lists: completed visa form; recent photo; original passport valid over 6 months plus previous passports; passport bio-page; NIC copy; birth certificate copy with English translation; applicant cover letter and study plan; school dean letter if applicable; degree certificates/transcripts certified by Sri Lanka's Ministry of Foreign Affairs; original personal bank statement for the last 3 months plus manager's balance confirmation issued within 14 days (if parents' account is used, parents' letter and ID copies); TB medical report from designated hospitals with full test results and doctor's letter; police report covering date of birth to present; Korean university admission certificate and tuition payment receipt; university scholastic aptitude/financial-capability letter if applicable; and language evidence. The Embassy list states TOPIK Level 3+ for Korean track, or official TOEFL/IELTS/TEPS certificate for English track. Certified documents must be valid within 3 months at application. Treat this as the current D-2 Sri Lanka checklist located during this review, and re-open it before each filing.

2. D-4 Korean-language study has a separate checklist.

Do not reuse the D-2 checklist for D-4-1. The VFS Korea Sri Lanka visa portal publishes a separate Korean Language Study D-4-1 checklist. Confirm the exact D-4 subtype, course duration, institution and current checklist, including its financial and academic requirements, before collecting documents. Verify genuine source and access to funds; never borrow a temporary balance or rely on future part-time wages as primary funding.

3. Part-time employment needs prior immigration permission.

D-2 and D-4 students are not free to work simply because they hold a student visa. The government-run Study in Korea employment guidance says students need the required Korean-language proficiency and permission from the local immigration office. Eligibility can depend on programme, language level, academic performance, attendance and status. The same official page currently states a maximum of 30 hours per week and says there is no unlimited-hours allowance during holidays or vacations under that guidance. Confirm the student's exact permitted hours and job category in the current permission before work starts; do not generalise university-specific or older rules.

4. Only permitted job categories and workplaces.

Permitted student jobs are generally limited to specified simple student-type activities, subject to the current rules and the student's permission. Some professional or specialised work requires a different legal basis. The student should obtain written guidance from the university international office and immigration before accepting an offer or changing workplace. Working without prior permission can create immigration consequences and affect future status applications.

5. Graduation and job-seeking status are separate.

Eligible graduates may explore a change to D-10 job-seeking status or another appropriate employment status, but this is a separate application with its own qualification, activity-plan, funds and other requirements. It is not automatic at graduation and does not guarantee a job. Check current HiKorea rules and the student's exact degree, graduation date and status before advising.

MALAYSIA

International student pass — EMGS, eVAL and post-arrival steps

Start with a genuine offer from an eligible institution. Higher-education applicants must have an offer from a public university or approved private higher education institution authorised to enrol international students. Institution and course eligibility must be checked, not assumed from a marketing brochure.

1. Apply through the institution / EMGS.

Malaysia Immigration states that higher-education applicants must be outside Malaysia when the application is submitted. The institution or student may submit through the relevant EMGS/STARS process. EMGS screens the application and eligible cases are forwarded for eVAL approval. Students from visa-required countries must then obtain the required Single Entry Visa through the official eVISA portal before travel.

2. Core application documents.

EMGS lists a white-background passport photo, passport bio-data and visa pages, passport validity of at least 18 months, offer letter and health declaration form. Academic records and English-language evidence are also relevant to the VAL process. The institution may request additional documents. Follow the applicant's institution-specific upload checklist and do not assume every applicant has an identical document set.

3. After arrival — medical and Student Pass endorsement.

Malaysia Immigration requires a post-arrival medical examination within 7 days. After the student passes the medical examination and the institution receives the EMGS support letter, the institution can submit the Student Pass endorsement application to the Immigration Office responsible for its location. Explain that arrival is not the final Student Pass endorsement.

4. Part-time work is restricted and needs approval.

Malaysia Immigration states that eligible Student Pass holders at public universities and private higher education institutions may apply to work up to 20 hours per week at approved locations such as restaurants, petrol stations, mini markets, hotels and university/college areas. Approval is discretionary and requires a job offer and institution support letter. Language-centre, training/skills-centre and accredited-centre students are not included in this work facility. Never promise that every student can work or use expected wages as proof of tuition/living funds.

5. Dependants are limited.

Immigration guidance permits eligible dependants only for Student Pass holders at Master's or PhD level. Do not tell diploma, foundation, bachelor's or language students that they can automatically bring dependants under this facility.

6. Budget and financial evidence.

The public Immigration and EMGS pages reviewed here do not set one single universal financial balance for every institution, programme and applicant. Obtain the latest EMGS/institution financial-document instructions and any Sri Lanka-specific visa requirements. Keep tuition, insurance, living costs, visa fees and sponsor funds separate in the budget; confirm the exact fee schedule before payment.

THAILAND

Non-Immigrant ED / ED Plus — study route and Sri Lanka e-Visa process

Choose the category by the real programme. Non-Immigrant ED is the education route for qualifying study, training and exchange activities. The Royal Thai Embassy in Colombo states that applicants in Sri Lanka and the Maldives must submit visa applications through the official Thai e-Visa platform; in-person passport submission is no longer the normal application channel. Its current general guidelines say applicants must remain physically in Sri Lanka or the Maldives throughout processing, apply at least one month before travel, upload clear PDF documents, and may be asked for additional documents or an interview. The Embassy says payment does not guarantee approval. Select the correct Non-Immigrant category and check the live category-specific upload list in the portal.

2. Admission and institution approval.

The sources reviewed did not establish one universal current financial threshold for all Sri Lankan ED/ED Plus applicants. Check the ED/ED Plus-specific live e-Visa document list and obtain any required amount, bank statement period, sponsor rules and accepted evidence from the Embassy or receiving institution. Record the exact source and date in the case file.

4. Initial stay and extensions.

Thai government guidance describes an initial stay of up to 90 days for a Non-Immigrant ED entrant, with an extension application through the Immigration Bureau supported by the institution and study records. Extensions are not automatic and are subject to immigration approval. Students must monitor their permitted-stay expiry and any applicable 90-day address-reporting obligation. If leaving Thailand while maintaining an eligible stay, confirm whether a re-entry permit is required before departure; leaving without one can affect the permission to stay.

5. Work is not an assumed student benefit.

Do not tell ED holders they can work part-time simply because they have a student visa. Thai government guidance says education visa holders are not permitted to work under the ED conditions. Any separate internship or employment must be checked for the correct immigration status and work authorisation before activity begins. Never use expected Thai earnings as the applicant's primary tuition or living-cost funding.

6. ED Plus post-graduation facility — not a work permit.

Thai government information describes ED Plus as a route for qualifying full-time bachelor's degree or higher students at Thai higher-education institutions, with a possible one-year stay extension after graduation to seek employment, travel or undertake other activities. This is not automatic for every student and does not itself authorise employment. If hired, the graduate must obtain the appropriate immigration status and work authorisation before working. Confirm implementation and eligibility with the university and Thai Immigration Bureau.

Student visa — work conditions

English-language ability and genuine study.

Counsellors should check the exact course/provider admission requirements and current INZ genuine-intention instructions; do not present this announcement as a new universal IELTS score threshold.

Financial evidence — tertiary students.

Verify the live INZ amount and the applicant's course category before quoting it.

If the programme is longer than one year, a credible plan for later tuition and living costs can strengthen the application; INZ says those later-year funds do not all need to be available immediately. The applicant must also show money or a ticket for onward travel, in addition to living funds, and hold acceptable medical/travel insurance for the required period.

Important for Sri Lankan applicants: INZ specifically says that citizens of Sri Lanka (among other listed South Asian countries) applying from South Asia may only use immediate family to sponsor them or provide a financial undertaking. Immediate family is defined on the INZ page as partner, parents, siblings, grandparents and parents-in-law. Do not assume a family friend or distant relative is acceptable for a Sri Lankan applicant applying from Sri Lanka; verify the precise sponsor/guarantor route and current evidence rules.

Funds Transfer Scheme (FTS) — Sri Lankan applicants: INZ lists Sri Lanka among the nationalities that may be able to use the FTS. Eligibility and age.

INZ states student work conditions can only be granted to students aged 16 or older. Tertiary students must meet the applicable full-time study/course criteria. Students must work for an employer and cannot be self-employed under these student work conditions.

Transition for older visas.

INZ says the allowed hours increased from 20 to 25 per week on 3 November 2025. A student whose earlier visa still states 20 hours may need to apply for a variation of conditions if eligible. Always check the actual eVisa or visa letter.

Post-study work pathway — do not promise eligibility from the course title.

A Post-Study Work Visa (PSWV) may allow up to 3 years, depending on the qualification and time studied in New Zealand. Current INZ criteria include a Level 7 degree studied full-time for at least 30 weeks in New Zealand; a Level 8–10 qualification studied full-time for at least 30 weeks; or a non-degree Level 4–7 qualification that appears on INZ's eligible list and is completed full-time in New Zealand for its full required duration. For non-degree Level 7 or lower qualifications, employment must relate to the study and the visa conditions specify the permitted work. Degree Level 7 or higher generally has open work conditions, subject to lawful work and any occupational registration.

Most applicants must apply within 3 months after their student visa expires; doctoral graduates have 6 months, and narrow consecutive-qualification situations can have 12 months. Applicants need at least NZD 5,000 for living expenses and must meet health, character and other requirements. The PSWV is generally available only once. Check the exact qualification, study duration, application deadline, visa history and current INZ list before recommending a course.

Important changes from 16 November 2026 — future effective date as at this review (13 November 2026).

INZ's published announcement says that from 16 November 2026, a new Short-Term Graduate Work Visa becomes available. English-language, foundation and bridging qualifications are excluded. Applicants need at least NZD 5,000, must meet health requirements, must not previously have held a PSWV or this new visa, and must apply within 3 months of their student visa expiring. Work must be for an employer under an employment agreement or contract for services; business ownership is not allowed. It cannot be extended or granted a second time.

Also from 16 November 2026, PSWV eligibility is extended to a person who completes an NZQCF Level 7 Graduate Diploma full-time in New Zealand for its full duration and also holds a bachelor's degree from New Zealand or overseas. Cross-crediting or recognition of prior learning does not count toward the required full-duration enrolment. The PSWV duration for this route is the time spent studying the Graduate Diploma, up to 1 year. A past PSWV holder is not eligible for another PSWV. Save the bachelor's degree certificate and official transcript. INZ also states paper applications for PSWV will no longer be accepted from 16 November 2026; apply online.

IRELAND

Non-EEA student pathway — visa, provider approval, funds, work and graduate permission

Scope: This summary is for non-EEA/non-Swiss applicants. Sri Lankan applicants should confirm their visa-required status and the exact application-office checklist for their place of residence. An Irish entry visa is not itself residence permission: the permission granted at the border and registered immigration stamp determine what the student may do.

1. Choose the correct visa by course duration.

For study longer than 90 days, a visa-required applicant needs a Long Stay “D” study visa. For study under 90 days, the relevant route is a Short Stay “C” visa; do not use a short-stay visit/study route as a substitute for long-term student residence. ISD says a long-stay study visa can be applied for up to 3 months before travel and the applicant must apply from their home country or a country where they are legally resident. Apply through AVATS and follow the submission instructions on the generated summary form.

2. Verify provider AND exact programme — ILEP / TrustEd Ireland.

Non-EEA/non-Swiss students may only be recruited to a programme listed on the Interim List of Eligible Programmes (ILEP), or an eligible programme offered by a provider authorised to use the TrustEd Ireland statutory quality mark. The ILEP is closed to new providers; its final update was published 20 June 2025. Existing ILEP-listed programmes may continue only while the provider remains compliant and the ILEP remains in operation. New providers must obtain TrustEd Ireland authorisation through QQI. TrustEd authorisation does not automatically make every course at that provider eligible: verify the exact course and applicable immigration criteria. Save a dated official register check, provider legal name, campus, programme title, NFQ level, full-time status, dates, award body and written offer.

Stop rule: if the exact programme is not on the current ILEP and the provider/course does not meet the TrustEd route, do not accept a deposit or describe it as eligible for Stamp 2. An agent partnership, college website or QQI mark alone is not enough to establish that every advertised programme is immigration-eligible.

3. Check admission, course and fee evidence.

The long-stay study visa acceptance letter must confirm full-time enrolment with at least 15 hours of organised daytime tuition each week, course details, total fees payable, fees paid and whether the college arranged medical insurance. For course fees below €6,000, the full fee must be paid before applying; where fees exceed €6,000, at least €6,000 must be paid before applying, with payment reflected in the acceptance letter. This is the immigration minimum; the college may require more or full payment. Use a traceable bank transfer or approved student-fee payment service and retain the official receipt; do not pay course fees in cash. The applicant must show academic ability and English/Irish language ability appropriate to the course. Explain any gaps in educational history and previous visa refusals accurately. Check the provider's published refund policy, learner-protection arrangements and all fees (including exam fees) before payment. ISD says programme fees must not be paid in cash.

4. Financial evidence — do not count future part-time work.

For academic courses beginning after 1 July 2023, visa-required applicants must show immediate access to at least €10,000 for one academic year, in addition to course fees. They must also show ready access to €10,000 for each subsequent year of study, in addition to tuition. For a course lasting less than 8 months, the requirement is €833 per month of stay or €6,665, whichever is lower. Funds must be available without relying on casual employment or public funds.

Provide the applicant's up-to-date bank statement showing six months of transactions. Explain large or irregular deposits and show source-of-funds evidence. If using a sponsor, identify every sponsor, prove the relationship, provide each sponsor's six-month bank history, employment confirmation and recent payslips or business evidence, and explain the amount and duration of support. Credit cards are not accepted as proof of funds. A savings/deposit account needs bank confirmation that funds can be withdrawn. Degree-programme students may be able to use the approved €10,000 education-bond route as an alternative; this is a pilot with specific provider/payment-service and holding conditions, not an option to assume for every course.

5. Private medical insurance and application file.

Private medical insurance is required for non-EEA students. The college may arrange it, but the acceptance letter must state this; otherwise the applicant must arrange and evidence their own cover. The long-stay visa checklist includes passport (normally valid at least 12 months after intended arrival), photographs, signed AVATS summary, application letter, acceptance letter, proof of fee payment, finances, academic ability, English/Irish ability, education-gap explanations and previous refusal details where relevant. Check the current local visa office instructions; additional documents or biometrics may be requested. Apply up to 3 months before travel, not earlier.

6. Stamp 2 conditions and work limits.

Eligible full-time students with Stamp 2 permission may work casual employment up to 20 hours per week during term time. The permitted 40-hour periods are June, July, August and September, and 15 December to 15 January. These are limited holiday periods, not a year-round 40-hour entitlement. Stamp 2A students cannot work. Students must not engage in self-employment or business. A visa sticker or college promise is not work permission: check the actual immigration stamp/IRP and its expiry date. Applicants must show sufficient funds without relying on work income.

Full-time English-language students must meet attendance and exam requirements: ISD's student guidance specifies at least 85% attendance and a recognised end-of-course examination. English-language study is limited to three eligible 25-week programmes (up to 8 months permission each), after which progression to an eligible higher-education programme is required for further student permission. Student-pathway time limits and academic progression must be checked.

7. Arrival, registration and family expectations.

A long-stay visa allows travel to Ireland; it does not guarantee entry or replace immigration permission. For stays over 90 days, the student must register their permission with ISD within 90 days. Stamp 2 conditions govern study and work. Student applicants should not assume spouse/children can accompany them: ISD says students are typically not permitted to bring family members, with only exceptional circumstances considered.

8. Third Level Graduate Programme (Stamp 1G).

Eligible graduates with an Irish NFQ Level 8 award may receive up to 12 months; eligible Level 9 or higher graduates may receive 12 months initially and a further 12 months subject to conditions. The graduate must hold current Stamp 2 and an up-to-date registration card, apply within 6 months of written notification of the award, provide the final award evidence, passport and medical insurance, and meet the overall student-permission time limits. Stamp 1G allows full-time employment while seeking graduate-level employment and an employment permit route, but does not permit self-employment or running a business. It is not a guaranteed job, employment permit or permanent-residence pathway.

Counsellor stop-rule: Do not quote a total budget without separating tuition, €10,000 living funds, later-year funding plan, travel, insurance, registration and other costs. Do not count projected part-time earnings. Do not promise work, visa approval, family accompaniment or Stamp 1G eligibility until the exact course, award level, provider route, applicant documents and current ISD rules are verified.

Student long-stay visa — admission, financial proof, work and arrival formalities

Scope: for a Sri Lankan applicant planning higher education or a course in France for more than 90 days, assess the long-stay student route (commonly VLS-TS étudiant for stays of up to one year). Do not count future part-time earnings as the initial visa financial proof.

The France-Visas “Job Search / Business Creation” route is available to a student who has left France after successfully obtaining at least a Master’s-level qualification from a nationally approved institution (or a programme on the prescribed list) and applies to return within four years of graduation. The route is not a job offer or automatic long-term work permit. France-Visas — Job Search / Business Creation.

A VLS-TS étudiant must be validated online within three months after arrival through the French authorities' ANEF portal. Service-Public currently lists a €100 tax payable when validating the student VLS-TS. Keep evidence of validation and payment. If the applicant's visa is a different long-stay category requiring a residence card application, follow the deadline printed on that visa rather than assuming the VLS-TS process applies.

France-Visas says non-EU students under 28 must register with the French social-security system. NETHERLANDS

Student residence permit — recognised sponsor, study norm, work and post-study route

Scope: this section focuses on international students applying for a Dutch university or higher professional education (HBO) programme. Do not assume the same route or financial amount applies to secondary or vocational (MBO) study. For a Sri Lankan national, confirm the current MVV/residence process and local embassy instructions with the Dutch institution and IND before filing.

1. Verify the institution and exact programme first.

Only an educational institution recognised by the IND as a sponsor can apply for the student residence permit for university/HBO study. Check the legal institution name in the live IND Public Register of Recognised Sponsors and confirm the exact campus and programme. The programme must be full-time and accredited by NVAO in the Netherlands or by an eligible quality-assurance route recognised by IND (including EQAR in Europe). An agent partnership, university marketing page or admission offer alone does not prove that the institution can sponsor the residence permit.

2. 2026 financial study norm — €1,130.77 per month.

For university/HBO students, the IND's published 2026 study norm is €1,130.77 per month, valid from 1 January to 31 December 2026. This is the living-cost norm and does not include tuition fees. The student must show sufficient funds for 12 months; the institution checks proof of income and the requirement is renewed each study year. For 12 months, the living-funds calculation is €13,569.24, before tuition and other costs. Recheck the IND table for the application year and study type.

Accepted evidence routes include the student's freely accessible bank funds; a deposit to the educational institution's account; an eligible scholarship; or financial support from a person abroad. For a private sponsor abroad, IND requires the prescribed Statement of financial support (private person), a copy of the sponsor's passport/ID and bank evidence in the sponsor's name showing at least 12 months of the study norm, free access to the funds and required bank details. These documents must generally be no older than 3 months. For funds in the student's own account, the account must be in the student's name, freely withdrawable, and the supporting bank document no older than 3 months; foreign bank accounts also require an original bank statement. For a sponsor resident in the Netherlands, the evidence route is different: prescribed support statement, municipal BRP extract showing household composition, ID and proof of independent, sustainable and sufficient income. Do not assume a family sponsorship letter alone is sufficient. Documents must be in Dutch, English, German or French, otherwise arrange translation.

3. Institution applies for MVV and residence permit.

The educational institution submits the student residence application and, where required, applies for the MVV entry visa and residence permit together. The IND page lists an application fee of €254 and a decision period of up to 60 days, which can be extended if the application is incomplete or further checks are needed. After approval, the student generally makes an appointment with the Dutch representation within three months for the MVV sticker. The passport must be valid for at least six more months when the sticker is placed; the MVV is valid for 90 days. Confirm the correct appointment location and collection instructions for Sri Lanka through NetherlandsWorldwide and the Dutch representation.

4. Maintain study progress — at least 50% of credits each study year.

The student must make sufficient progress and normally obtain at least 50% of the credits for each study year. The institution assesses progress and reports insufficient progress to the IND unless there are valid reasons under the applicable rules. A residence permit can be affected if the student does not meet the study-progress requirement. Counsellors should not describe the permit as guaranteed for the full course regardless of academic performance.

5. Work during study — 16 hours/week OR full-time in June, July and August.

For employee work, the employer must obtain a Dutch work permit (TWV). The student must choose either up to 16 hours per week during the year, or full-time work in June, July and August. These are alternatives, not an entitlement to combine 16 hours weekly with unlimited summer work. A traineeship that is part of the study programme does not need a TWV. IND also states that self-employment may be possible without a TWV while the student continues to meet residence-permit requirements, but registration with the Chamber of Commerce and tax/health-insurance obligations may apply. Never promise that a job will be found or that part-time earnings can cover tuition or initial proof of funds.

6. Post-study orientation year — eligibility is limited, not automatic.

Eligible graduates may apply for a one-year orientation-year residence permit to seek work or start a business. It can cover, among other categories, graduates of accredited Dutch bachelor/master programmes, certain Dutch post-master programmes, Erasmus Mundus Joint Masters, qualifying research, and specified foreign master's/doctoral/post-master qualifications from designated institutions. For a qualifying foreign institution, IND applies top-200 ranking tests across specified ranking publishers and additional conditions, including an English-language requirement such as IELTS 6.0 or an accepted equivalent unless an exemption applies. Foreign diplomas need Nuffic evaluation. The orientation-year permit is valid for one year and cannot be extended. Do not promise this route to every Dutch graduate or every overseas degree holder.

HUNGARY

Study residence permit — eligibility, funds, work and post-study route

Scope: Third-country nationals, including Sri Lankan applicants, seeking full-time study at a state-recognised Hungarian higher-education institution or an authorised foreign higher-education institution operating in Hungary. A qualifying preparatory course may also be covered. A generic short course should not be assumed eligible.

1. Admission and application route.

Obtain the institution's admission/student-status certificate and evidence of the language skills required for the programme. Tuition-fee payment evidence is a mandatory attachment. Visa-required applicants normally apply for the study residence permit through the competent Hungarian diplomatic/consular mission; the residence-permit application also includes the entry visa to collect the permit. If approved, the D visa is a single-entry visa valid for 3 months and authorises a stay of up to 30 days for permit collection/entry formalities. Confirm the filing channel with the Hungarian mission responsible for Sri Lanka.

2. Financial evidence — no single fixed euro amount published in the student factsheet.

The National Directorate-General for Aliens Policing (NDGAP/OIF) requires sufficient legally acquired resources to cover subsistence, accommodation, return/onward travel and healthcare. Evidence may include the applicant's bank certificate/statements, savings and transaction history, regular income, a notarised family-support declaration with evidence of the sponsor's ability, or scholarship evidence. Do not quote an agent's fixed monthly amount as a universal national minimum. Accommodation address and comprehensive health cover/capacity to pay healthcare costs must also be evidenced.

3. Permit duration and renewal.

The study residence permit is generally issued for at least 1 year (or the shorter course duration) and is capped at 3 years per permit; extensions may be granted for 1–3 years. An extension is filed in Hungary through Enter Hungary, while the existing permit remains valid, no earlier than 90 days before expiry and no later than 30 days before expiry. Academic progress and continued enrolment are relevant.

4. Work during studies.

With a study-purpose residence permit, a third-country national may work up to 30 hours per week during the study period, and full-time outside the study period for up to 90 days in a year. This is a legal maximum, not a job guarantee; the student must maintain study progress and comply with employment rules.

5. After graduation.

A graduate may apply for a residence permit to seek a job or start a business only while still holding a valid study-purpose residence permit, after successfully completing the studies, and where the intended job/business is appropriate to the level of the completed studies. This is a separate application, not an automatic extension or guaranteed job placement. Confirm the current application window and requirements in the OIF job-seeking factsheet.

FINLAND

Student residence permit — funds, insurance, work and post-study options

Scope: Non-EU/EEA applicant applying for a first residence permit for degree/vocational studies in Finland. A student visa is not the normal route for degree-length study: the applicant generally needs a residence permit for studies before travelling.

1. Study place and application route.

Obtain an acceptance from an eligible Finnish educational institution for a degree, vocational qualification or qualifying exchange. Apply for the first residence permit from abroad, normally through Enter Finland; then prove identity and present originals at the Finnish mission or designated service point as instructed. Mainly online/distance studies that do not require residence in Finland do not qualify for a student residence permit. Admission alone does not guarantee a permit.

2. Living funds — first application.

For studies lasting at least one year, show €9,600 for the first year (€800/month). For shorter studies, show €800 per month. This is separate from tuition. If tuition is unpaid at application time, the applicant must also have enough funds for the first-year tuition in addition to living funds. Living-cost money cannot also be counted as tuition. An approved grant/scholarship for living costs can reduce the cash amount. Institution-provided free accommodation may reduce the requirement to €400/month; free accommodation and meals may reduce it to €270/month, with written proof.

3. Bank and sponsor evidence.

Attach a bank statement covering the previous six months, showing account holder, bank and currency. Funds must be in the applicant's own personal-use account and accessible in Finland. A relative's sponsorship promise/agreement or sponsor bank statement alone is not accepted as proof of the student's funds. If family transferred funds to the student, Migri may request evidence of the money's origin, such as sponsor tax records or asset/bank evidence. Do not advise artificial last-minute deposits or assume a family sponsorship letter substitutes for the applicant's own funds.

4. Health insurance.

Insurance must be valid on arrival and throughout the stay, with excess no more than €300. For studies lasting less than two years, coverage for medical expenses must be at least €120,000. For studies lasting two years or more, pharmaceutical expenses must be covered up to at least €40,000. Ordinary travel insurance limited to 90-day trips is not sufficient. Check the actual policy wording, coverage period and deductible.

5. Application fee and documents.

Migri's study-permit page currently lists €600 for an electronic first application and €750 for a paper first application (minor applicants have separate fees). Core documents include passport, acceptance certificate, six-month bank statement, insurance certificate, proof of paid tuition or scholarship and any requested education/employment records. Fees can change; check the live Migri fee table before payment.

6. Student work rights.

With a valid study residence permit, paid employment in any field is limited to an average of 30 hours per week over the year; some weeks can exceed 30 if the annual average stays within the limit. Practical training or diploma work that is part of the degree may be done without this 30-hour restriction. Work must not prevent normal study progress. Crucially, for the first residence permit, the applicant cannot rely on future work to meet the initial income requirement.

7. Extensions and post-study route.

For a study-permit extension, Migri checks study progress; higher-education students are generally expected to complete approximately 45 credits per academic year, or explain lower progress. Work income may be considered for an extension if evidenced. After completing a Finnish degree, an eligible graduate may apply for a residence permit to look for work or start a business for up to two years; a separate permit based on a completed Finnish degree is available where a job contract or binding offer exists. Do not promise automatic post-study residence or employment.

SWEDEN

Higher-education residence permit — admission, funds, work and post-study route

Scope: Non-EU/EEA applicant seeking a first residence permit for on-site higher education in Sweden. For stays under three months, a residence permit is generally not the route; check entry-visa requirements separately. This section does not cover doctoral employment permits or every vocational/other-study category.

1. Admission and application.

Applicant needs a valid passport and final admission to full-time, on-site studies. At a university/university college, 30 credits per semester counts as full-time. Distance learning does not qualify for this higher-education student residence permit. Any tuition fee must be paid before applying; the institution notifies the Swedish Migration Agency (Migrationsverket). First-time application is generally made while outside Sweden, and the permit must be issued before travel.

2. Financial maintenance — applications in 2026.

Minimum SEK 10,656 per month for the period requested. For 12 months this is SEK 127,872. This is a living-cost threshold, separate from tuition. Documented free food can reduce the monthly requirement by SEK 2,960; documented free accommodation by SEK 4,736. If both are genuinely provided, the combined reduction may apply under Migrationsverket's conditions. Family members accompanying the student increase the required amount.

3. Bank funds and family support.

Personal bank assets must be in an account belonging to the applicant (and accessible to the applicant); the bank statement must be issued no more than four months before the requested permit start date. Private individuals such as parents are not accepted as formal sponsors. Family money must be transferred to an account in the student's name and accessible to them; Migrationsverket may require evidence of the source. A private sponsor letter or parent bank statement alone is not enough. Institutional/organisation sponsorship, scholarships, eligible grants or loans, and evidenced employment income can be assessed under Migrationsverket's specified documentation rules. A job offer alone is insufficient; employment certificate and payslips are required when relying on work income.

4. Health insurance.

For a stay under one year, comprehensive health insurance is required for the full stay, covering emergency and other medical care, hospitalisation, emergency dental care and medical repatriation. For a stay of at least one year, the student should register in the Swedish Population Register and then access the national healthcare arrangements. Verify policy terms and dates, not just the insurance certificate title.

5. Student work rights — major 2026 change.

For bachelor's/master's-level students whose residence permit was granted on or after 11 June 2026, paid work is generally limited to 15 hours per week during semesters. Work may be unrestricted in June, July and August, and certain education-related internships, student representation/administration, and qualifying university research work are exempt. A student who works beyond the permitted limit risks permit revocation or refusal of an extension. Students granted permits before 11 June 2026 are generally transitional-exempt from the 15-hour cap until they extend. Check the grant date and actual permit status; never promise unrestricted part-time work.

6. Study progress and address notification.

For first-/second-cycle higher-education permits, the tightened acceptable progress rule from 11 June 2026 is 37.5 credits during the first year and 45 credits per year from the second year onwards. Insufficient progress can affect extension or lead to revocation. Students must notify Migrationsverket of their address within 30 days after receiving a permit and after moving, unless population registration applies. The address-notification change generally does not apply to permits granted before 11 June 2026 until extension.

7. Application fee and post-study option.

Current adult application fee shown by Migrationsverket: SEK 1,500 (children under 18: SEK 750); fees are non-refundable if refused. After completing an eligible higher-education programme lasting at least two semesters and passing all courses, a graduate may apply before the current permit expires for a permit to seek work or explore starting a business. This is up to one year after bachelor's/master's studies and one to 18 months after doctoral studies, subject to funds, insurance and other requirements. It is not automatic and does not guarantee a job.

Higher-education student residence permit — admission, funds, work and post-study

Scope: Non-EU applicant admitted full-time to an eligible state-approved Danish higher-education programme. Other course types and non-state-approved programmes can have materially different rights.

Applicant must understand the language of instruction sufficiently to participate. SIRI's published normal processing time is two months (may take up to three); current processing fee shown is DKK 3,060.

2. Financial self-support — 2026.

Show disposable funds of DKK 7,426 per month for the intended study period, up to 12 months. For a course longer than one year, the maximum 12-month amount is DKK 89,112. Evidence must be liquid funds accessible to the applicant; bank statement must be no more than 30 days old and show name, date, balance and currency. A parent’s bank statement or a simple third-party support letter is not accepted as the applicant's own funds. A granted scholarship or student loan may be accepted. If tuition is payable and first-semester tuition has already been paid, SIRI accepts this as evidence of sufficient funds under its stated rule.

3. Student work rights — state-approved programmes.

Students in state-approved higher-education programmes receive a limited work permit: 90 hours per month from September to May and full-time in June, July and August. A mandatory internship that forms part of the programme can have a separate full-time work permission. Students in non-state-approved programmes who applied on or after 2 May 2025 generally do not receive student work permission; check the programme's exact status.
