UK · AUSTRALIA · NEW ZEALAND

Provider and course eligibility — verify the exact enrolment

Do not approve a destination or course based only on an agent brochure, university logo, partner claim or the fact that an institution is well known. Verify the institution, campus, exact course, delivery arrangement and the visa-specific enrolment document against the responsible official register.

United Kingdom — licensed Student sponsor + eligible course + CAS.

Search the current GOV.UK Register of licensed sponsors: Students. Match the legal institution name, sponsor category and rating; check the register again close to application because a licence can change. A sponsor being listed is not by itself enough: verify the exact course meets the Student route requirements, the qualification and level rules, study location and any relevant ATAS requirement. Obtain the CAS issued for that applicant and course; check the CAS details, fees paid/accommodation, course dates, place of study and sponsor licence status. GOV.UK says the visa application must be made within 6 months of receiving the CAS. Do not treat a conditional offer, agent confirmation or deposit receipt as a CAS.

Stop rule: if the institution is absent from the live register, the course or delivery site cannot be reconciled with the CAS, or the CAS details are incorrect/withdrawn, stop and refer to the institution and compliance reviewer before filing.

Australia — CRICOS provider + exact CRICOS course + valid CoE.

Search the Australian Government CRICOS register for both the provider and the exact course. Match the CRICOS provider code, course CRICOS code, campus/location, course duration, delivery mode and current registration dates. CRICOS registration is the official register for courses/providers permitted to enrol overseas students on student visas. Then obtain and check the Confirmation of Enrolment (CoE) for every intended course. Home Affairs requires a valid CoE for the intended full-time course(s) at application; a cancelled or completed CoE is no longer valid, and packaged courses need all relevant CoE codes included. Recheck CoE validity when the Department decides the application. Do not assume a provider's CRICOS registration covers every course or campus.

Stop rule: if the provider or exact course/campus is not currently CRICOS registered, the course code differs from the offer, or the CoE is cancelled/invalid, do not proceed on the basis of an agent or provider marketing page.

New Zealand — approved provider + Code signatory + offer of place.

Check the education provider's status and whether it is a signatory to the Education (Pastoral Care of Tertiary and International Learners) Code of Practice 2021 using the official NZQA information/register. Immigration New Zealand says international students cannot study at an institution that is not a Code signatory. Check the exact campus, qualification/programme and offer of place; the provider must assess that the course is appropriate to the student's expectations and that the student has the English ability and academic capability to pass. Confirm tuition, course dates, full-time/part-time status, insurance arrangements and the current visa requirements. Provider Code signatory status is a required provider check, not a promise of visa approval.

Stop rule: if the provider's Code status, programme approval/quality status, campus or offer details cannot be confirmed, pause the recommendation and obtain written clarification from the provider/NZQA or escalate to compliance.

Evidence to save for every destination: date/time checked; official register URL and saved evidence; legal provider name; provider ID/code; campus; exact course title/code; qualification/level; delivery mode; start/end dates; enrolment document (CAS/CoE/offer); reviewer and decision. Recheck before application because register status and course details can change.

UAE / DUBAI

Student residence pathway

Core distinction: a student visa / residence permit is an immigration status for study. It should not be presented as an automatic general work permit or a guarantee of part-time employment.

1. Confirm the institution and sponsorship route.

The UAE Government describes student residence sponsorship through a UAE-resident parent or the accredited university/college. GDRFA Dubai says an enrolled foreign student at an accredited institution is hosted/guaranteed by the educational institution. Confirm the actual sponsor and emirate with the institution before quoting a process.

2. Check the institution's recognition and eligibility.

GDRFA lists licensing of the institution and approval by the Ministry of Education or competent emirate education authority among its conditions. Do not assume every training centre or course qualifies.

3. Prepare the likely residence documents.

GDRFA lists a recent white-background photo, passport copy, approved medical examination, ID receipt and health insurance. Visit Dubai's study guidance additionally lists an admission/study certificate, student undertaking and other documents depending on the applicant. Use the institution's current written checklist for the actual application.

4. Verify the fees before making a client quotation.

The GDRFA service page currently displays AED 200 residence permit fee, AED 10 Knowledge Dirham, AED 10 Innovation Dirham, AED 500 inside-country fee (where applicable) and AED 20 delivery. It also states an additional AED 100 per year when residency exceeds two years. These are service-page fee components, not a universal all-in student budget. Confirm applicability and any updated charges in the live application channel.

5. Check validity, renewal and expiry conditions.

The GDRFA Dubai student-permit service page states an expected completion time of 48 hours and a 60-day grace period after expiry or cancellation for the service described. Do not generalise this to every UAE permit category.

The Federal ICP renewal service page separately lists a 180-day grace period for students after completing their studies, and a 60-day grace period for residence permits issued with a guarantor or host. These are distinct categories; do not tell every student that they automatically receive 180 days. Confirm the applicant's emirate, sponsor/host, whether studies have been completed, permit category and live authority instructions before advising.

6. Explain work carefully.

Never promise a job or assume student residence alone authorises employment. MoHRE publishes a Student Training and Employment Permit framework for establishments registered with the Ministry. Its published requirements include proof of student status, a valid UAE residence visa for expatriate students, Emirates ID, and other conditions (including consent / institute NOC where applicable). Confirm the current permit type, age and eligibility, employer jurisdiction (MoHRE or relevant free zone), and approval before any work begins.

AUSTRALIA

Student visa subclass 500 — work limits and rule checks

Work cap while study is in session: the Department of Home Affairs currently states that most Student visa holders may work up to 48 hours per fortnight while their course is in session. The fortnight is a rolling 14-day period starting on a Monday; calculate the total across all jobs, not separately for each employer.

Before course commencement.

Student visa holders generally cannot work before their course starts, unless they held another visa with work permission at the time they applied. Confirm the actual visa conditions and grant date.

During scheduled course breaks.

Students may generally work unlimited hours when the course is not in session. A scheduled break must be confirmed against the provider's official academic calendar; do not assume every gap between classes is a holiday.

Exceptions and placements.

Students undertaking a masters by research or doctoral degree may have no work-hour limit after the relevant course has commenced. A work placement can be excluded from the cap only when it is a mandatory, registered component of the CRICOS course and within the required period. Do not apply this exception to optional placements or ordinary paid work experience.

Individual conditions override a generic summary.

Check the visa grant notice and VEVO for the applicant's actual conditions (including condition 8105), and verify the current legislative instrument before advising. A work-hour limit is not a job offer or guarantee of employment.

2026 legislative instrument review

The Federal Register details page for F2026L01347 displays a metadata effective date of 1 October 2026. However, section 2 of the original instrument states that it commences the day after registration, i.e. 2 October 2026. Record both dates separately and use the operative commencement clause when assessing application timing. The amendments apply to visa applications made on or after commencement; review the exact amended clauses and transitional scope before applying them to a client.

CANADA

Study permit — financial evidence and student work

Financial evidence: IRCC requires proof of enough money, without working in Canada, for tuition, living expenses for the applicant and accompanying family members, and transportation to and from Canada. Applicants must show first-year funds and explain how they will pay for the full duration of longer programmes. IRCC assesses both the amount and the source of funds.

Living-cost amount for applications received on or after 1 September 2026.

For applicants outside Quebec, the current IRCC table is:

  • 1 person: CAD 23,448
  • 2 people: CAD 29,192
  • 3 people: CAD 35,888
  • 4 people: CAD 43,572
  • 5 people: CAD 49,419
  • 6 people: CAD 55,736
  • 7 people: CAD 62,054
  • Each additional family member: CAD 6,318

Count the applicant and accompanying family members. These amounts exclude tuition and transportation. Quebec has separate provincial financial requirements; do not apply this table to Quebec applicants. Use the table that matches the actual application-received date.

Evidence and source of funds.

IRCC examples include tuition/housing payment receipts (with separate living/travel funds), education loans, scholarships, Canadian bank account evidence, GICs, foreign or Canadian bank statements for the past 6 months, pension documents, rental income, and a support letter with the supporter's occupation, relationship, dependants and amount of support. Support letters should be accompanied by relationship evidence and the supporter's ID or business registration. Bank statements should explain the source of deposits/income. These are examples, not an exhaustive checklist; check the applicant's local visa-office instructions. If foreign-exchange controls apply, evidence may be needed that funds can be exported.

PAL / TAL — 2026 study-permit cap and exemptions.

Most applicants to study in a Canadian province or territory need a Provincial Attestation Letter (PAL) or Territorial Attestation Letter (TAL). The province/territory issues it, usually through the DLI. Submit it with the study permit application; do not plan to submit it later. IRCC says a PAL/TAL for the 2026 cap year is generally valid until 31 December 2026 unless it has an earlier expiry date. A PAL/TAL issued in a previous cap year cannot be used for a 2026 application.

From 1 January 2026, applicants entering a degree-granting master's or doctoral programme at a public DLI are exempt from PAL/TAL. The exemption is not a blanket exemption for all graduate certificates, private colleges, or every programme described as postgraduate. Check that the institution is on IRCC's public-DLI graduate-program list and that the programme is degree-granting. Primary and secondary students and certain other listed categories are also exempt.

Quebec: a PAL/TAL is not the whole provincial requirement. Most students who do not need a PAL/TAL still need a valid Quebec Acceptance Certificate (CAQ), unless a specific Quebec exemption applies. Check the CAQ validity and wording with the Quebec authority and DLI.

Changes after a PAL/TAL: a new PAL/TAL is generally needed when changing schools or levels of study, with stated exceptions. An extension at the same DLI and same level is exempt; restoring student status requires a new PAL/TAL. A move from post-secondary study to a degree-granting master's/doctoral programme at a public DLI is also treated as an exemption. Check IRCC's current scenario table before reusing a letter.

Sri Lanka application checklist — check regional instructions.

IRCC's Colombo visa-office checklist (IMM 5864 E, February 2026) asks applicants residing in Sri Lanka to provide evidence of accumulated funds with a history of at least 6 months, including bank records showing balances and a written explanation of the source of funds. It also requests a one-page study plan addressing the programme choice, educational goal, why a similar programme is not being pursued in the country of residence/citizenship, and how the programme may improve employment opportunities there; explain education or career gaps where relevant. This is a local checklist: confirm the latest version and applicability to the applicant's place of residence before filing.

Off-campus work during regular study periods.

Eligible students may work up to 24 hours per week during regular academic sessions. The student must meet IRCC eligibility and study-permit conditions, including eligible DLI/full-time enrolment requirements and having started the programme. Students enrolled only in ESL/FSL, general-interest courses or prerequisite courses are not eligible for off-campus work under these rules. More than 24 hours during regular sessions can breach permit conditions.

Scheduled breaks.

Eligible students may work unlimited hours during a scheduled break only if it is scheduled by the school and lasts at least 7 consecutive days. IRCC states a maximum of 180 days of unlimited off-campus work during a calendar year; special limits apply to back-to-back breaks longer than 150 days. A statutory holiday or long weekend by itself is not a scheduled break. Confirm the official DLI calendar and permit conditions.

Co-op / internship placements.

Work placements have separate eligibility rules. IRCC says post-secondary students no longer need a co-op work permit for placements from 1 April 2026, subject to the applicable eligibility framework. Confirm the placement is a required component of the programme and review current IRCC instructions before advising.

Post-Graduation Work Permit (PGWP) — check before recommending a Canadian course.

PGWP is not automatic after graduation. The applicant must complete an eligible programme at a PGWP-eligible DLI. IRCC's general criteria currently include a programme at least 8 months long (900 hours for Quebec programmes), full-time status in each semester (part-time may be allowed in the final semester), and applying within 180 days after confirmation of completion; the study permit must have been valid at some point during the 180 days after completion. Check exceptions and the applicant's exact study history.

Language and field-of-study rules: bachelor's, master's and doctoral graduates need CLB 7 / NCLC 7 in all four language skills and have no field-of-study restriction. Other university graduates also need CLB 7 / NCLC 7; if their study-permit application was made on or after 1 November 2024, their programme must be in an eligible field. College, polytechnic and other non-university graduates need CLB 5 / NCLC 5 in all four skills; the same 1 November 2024 study-permit application-date test applies to the eligible-field requirement. Check the current eligible-field list and exact programme/CIP code before giving advice.

High-risk programme checks: English/French as a second language, general-interest/self-improvement and non-credit programmes do not qualify. Many public-private curriculum-licensing partnership programmes are not PGWP-eligible. A DLI's presence on the DLI list alone does not prove that the particular programme qualifies. Distance-learning rules, school transfers and authorised leave can also affect eligibility and permit length.

Length: never promise a three-year PGWP. The permit length depends on programme level, length and applicable IRCC rules; verify the specific case using the official IRCC guidance before quoting any expected duration.

Study-permit refusal-risk review — evidence, not a prediction.

Before submission, reconcile the applicant's study plan with previous education and work history, explain course choice and career relevance, demonstrate credible funds from traceable sources without relying on future Canadian work, confirm the DLI/programme/LOA and PAL/TAL or exemption (and CAQ where relevant), and check identity, travel/immigration history, biometrics, medical and other document requirements for the applicant. Explain gaps, changes of direction, dependants and unusual deposits with evidence. No checklist can predict or guarantee approval; respond to the actual refusal reasons if a previous application was refused.

2026 CIP code verification — do not rely on course title alone.

For non-degree programmes where the field-of-study rule applies, obtain the programme's exact 6-digit CIP 2021 code in writing from the DLI and independently match it to IRCC's current eligible list. A marketing title such as “IT”, “business”, “healthcare” or “management” is not enough to establish the CIP classification. IRCC states that a CIP code must be eligible when the applicant submits the study permit application or the PGWP application; the application-date rules and any grandfathering must be checked for that individual. IRCC has announced that the PGWP eligible-field list is frozen for 2026, meaning no eligible fields will be added or removed during 2026. Save the dated official list and the institution's written CIP confirmation to the client file.

Course-selection stop rule: if the DLI cannot confirm the exact CIP code, the code does not match the programme content, or the code is not eligible for the applicant's relevant application date, do not market the course as PGWP-eligible. Escalate for compliance review before accepting a client deposit or issuing a recommendation.

PGWP duration — never promise a fixed number of years.

The minimum programme length to meet the general eligibility threshold is 8 months (900 hours for Quebec programmes), but the PGWP validity period is a separate calculation. It depends on the programme credential, official programme length, eligible study time in Canada and the current IRCC rules. Online study outside Canada, school transfers and certain study periods may reduce or affect the permit. Do not quote “3 years” based only on a college brochure; check the current IRCC duration guidance for the exact credential and study history.

DLI + exact programme verification — complete before recommending or taking a deposit.

Use IRCC's live Designated Learning Institutions (DLI) list. Search the exact institution name, province, city and campus; record the DLI number and confirm the intended campus is covered. Then open the separate “DLIs offering PGWP-eligible programs” list and check whether the institution appears there. IRCC expressly warns that not every programme offered by a listed DLI is PGWP-eligible; inspect the eligible-programme details for that institution rather than relying on the institution name or a recruiter statement.

Verify the exact programme in writing with the institution: full official programme title; credential awarded; campus and delivery location; start date and expected duration; full-time or part-time status; in-person / online / blended delivery; whether the programme is delivered directly by the DLI or through a private/public partnership; and, where the field-of-study rule applies, the exact six-digit CIP 2021 code. Ask the DLI to confirm in writing that this exact programme and campus are included in its current PGWP-eligible programme details. Save the dated reply and the official IRCC list evidence in the client file.

Cross-check separately: (1) DLI status; (2) whether the DLI appears in the PGWP-eligible DLI list; (3) whether the exact programme is listed as eligible; (4) the student's credential level and study-permit application date for language / field-of-study rules; (5) the six-digit CIP code where required; and (6) general PGWP criteria such as programme length, full-time study history, distance learning, transfers and authorised leave. These are separate tests, not interchangeable approvals.

Stop and escalate: if the school cannot identify the exact campus or programme, the programme is absent from the eligible-programme details, the CIP code is unclear or mismatched, or the institution's written answer conflicts with IRCC, do not advertise a PGWP outcome or accept a deposit on the promise of post-study work. Escalate for compliance review and explain the uncertainty to the client. Even a programme that appears eligible does not guarantee a PGWP; the graduate must meet all rules in force when applying.

EUROPE / SCHENGEN

Do not treat Schengen as one student-visa system

The common Schengen short-stay rules do not replace national long-stay student visa and residence-permit rules. The host country decides the long-stay study process.

Study up to 90 days

Check whether a short-stay Schengen visa is required and apply to the country responsible for the main destination. A short-stay visa is not a substitute for a national student residence permit for a longer course.

Study longer than 90 days

Use the national long-stay visa / student residence procedure of the country where the institution is located. Financial means, accommodation, insurance, admission and other evidence vary by country.

Germany — Sri Lanka example

For a Sri Lankan applicant planning long-term study in Germany, use the German Embassy Colombo's national-visa student instructions and online portal. The Embassy's student page lists items including passport, application forms, motivation letter/CV, academic certificates, admission, language evidence and proof of subsistence. It currently displays a blocked-account figure of EUR 11,904 per year / EUR 992 per month; verify the live figure and accepted alternatives before counselling because financial thresholds can change.

The Embassy says student applications are submitted through the Federal Foreign Office online portal for preliminary review, followed by an in-person appointment when instructed. The current 2026 blocked-account amount is also shown by the federal “Make it in Germany” portal; the Embassy page should remain the applicant-specific checklist and final authority for Colombo. Do not confuse its separate short-stay Schengen visitor process with the national student visa route.

Student work rights.

The Federal Government's Make it in Germany portal states that students may work up to 140 full days or 280 half days per year, or up to 20 hours per week. It also lists the 2026 blocked-account minimum as EUR 11,904. Check the current residence-permit conditions, statutory rules, permitted counting method and latest financial amount before advising; do not treat work as guaranteed income or as a substitute for required proof of funds.

NEW ZEALAND

Student visa — work conditions

Immigration New Zealand says eligible student visa holders may be allowed to work up to 25 hours per week, with full-time work during specified study breaks/holiday periods where the visa conditions allow it. The work entitlement depends on the course and the individual eVisa or visa letter; it is not automatic for every student visa.

Important change from 27 October 2026 — Master's by research.

INZ removed unlimited in-study work rights for new master's-by-research students. New master's students are subject to the standard 25-hours-per-week in-study limit and full-time work during eligible holidays, where their visa conditions permit. INZ states this change applies to new student visa applications made on or after 27 October 2026. Do not apply this new rule automatically to earlier applications or already-granted visas; check the application date and individual eVisa/visa letter.

English-language ability and genuine study.

INZ's 27 October 2026 announcement says English-language ability is a key factor in assessing whether a student can meet visa conditions. Counsellors should check the exact course/provider admission requirements and current INZ genuine-intention instructions; do not present this announcement as a new universal IELTS score threshold.

Student visa end date after course completion.

INZ says the post-course end-date period is extended from 1 month to 2 months for the relevant student visas. The 31 March expiry date remains unchanged. Check the visa's actual expiry date and the course/duration category rather than calculating an assumed date for a client.

Financial evidence — tertiary students.

INZ's current Fee Paying Student Visa page requires tertiary, English-language and other non-compulsory students to show NZD 20,000 for each year of study (if studying for 1 year or more), or NZD 1,667 per month for study shorter than 1 year. Compulsory-school students have a separate NZD 17,000/year or NZD 1,417/month figure. This is living-cost evidence, separate from tuition and outward travel. Verify the live INZ amount and the applicant's course category before quoting it.

Tuition evidence must cover 1 course or 1 year of study, whichever is shorter. If the programme is longer than one year, a credible plan for later tuition and living costs can strengthen the application; INZ says those later-year funds do not all need to be available immediately. The applicant must also show money or a ticket for onward travel, in addition to living funds, and hold acceptable medical/travel insurance for the required period.

INZ lists evidence such as bank statements, paid accommodation, scholarship evidence, qualifying education-loan documents, fixed-term deposits, sponsor sharing ID, or a completed Financial Undertaking for a Student. A guarantor must show funds that are their own and not borrowed for the undertaking. INZ's INZ 1014 form requests evidence including the relationship, identity and funds; its form gives the tertiary maintenance rate as NZD 20,000/year or NZD 1,667/month.

Important for Sri Lankan applicants: INZ specifically says that citizens of Sri Lanka (among other listed South Asian countries) applying from South Asia may only use immediate family to sponsor them or provide a financial undertaking. Immediate family is defined on the INZ page as partner, parents, siblings, grandparents and parents-in-law. Do not assume a family friend or distant relative is acceptable for a Sri Lankan applicant applying from Sri Lanka; verify the precise sponsor/guarantor route and current evidence rules.

Eligibility and age.

INZ states student work conditions can only be granted to students aged 16 or older. Tertiary students must meet the applicable full-time study/course criteria. Students must work for an employer and cannot be self-employed under these student work conditions.

Transition for older visas.

INZ says the allowed hours increased from 20 to 25 per week on 3 November 2025. A student whose earlier visa still states 20 hours may need to apply for a variation of conditions if eligible. Always check the actual eVisa or visa letter.

DAILY MONITOR ALERT TRIAGE

How to review a possible page-text change

A monitor alert is a lead for a counsellor, not a legal conclusion. Text fingerprints can change because of page redesigns, navigation, cookie notices, updated timestamps, translations or other dynamic content. Never copy an alert directly into client advice.

1. Open the exact official source.

Use the source link shown in VisaWatch Update Monitor. Confirm that it is the responsible authority for the applicant's destination and visa category.

2. Compare the relevant rule, not just the page.

Find the exact paragraph, condition, fee table or legal instrument that changed. Check linked legislation, policy instructions and transition provisions where relevant. If the page only changed layout or navigation, record “No substantive rule change identified” rather than a policy alert.

3. Capture the dates separately.

Record the page publication/updated date, the legal instrument registration date (if any), the commencement/effective date, the date the change applies to applications or permits, and the date checked. If a date is not stated, write “Not stated” rather than estimating it.

4. Check who the change applies to.

Confirm nationality, application location, application date, visa subclass/category, course and provider, family/dependant situation, existing visa conditions, exceptions and transitional rules. Do not assume a general page applies to every client.

5. Record the human review decision.

Use the company's approved case/audit record to capture source URL, exact wording or evidence reference, previous wording, new wording, substantive impact, affected applicants, reviewer name, review date, decision and follow-up action. Keep the rule “Pending human review” until this is complete.

6. Escalate uncertainty.

If the official wording is unclear, pages conflict, or a legal interpretation is needed, escalate to the authorised immigration compliance/legal reviewer or the responsible authority. Do not resolve uncertainty by guessing.

System limitation: VisaWatch currently detects source availability and possible page-text changes. This reference page does not itself save reviewer decisions or provide a persistent approval/audit log. Record review outcomes in the company's approved record system until a secure, access-controlled review workflow is implemented.
