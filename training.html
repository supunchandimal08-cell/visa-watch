Lesson 1 — The safe counselling workflow

  1. Initial enquiry: understand why the client wants to study abroad. Do not promise a visa, job or permanent residence.
  2. Client assessment: record age range, nationality/residence, highest qualification, grades, English status, study gaps, previous refusals, budget, goal and intake.
  3. Course and provider research: confirm the provider is officially recognised for the relevant visa route; check course level, duration, entry requirements, fees and refund terms.
  4. Visa rule verification: use the official immigration authority. Record source title, URL, checked date, publication date, effective date, applicant scope and exceptions.
  5. Financial assessment: separate tuition, living costs, visa/insurance, travel and emergency reserve. Do not assume part-time earnings will fund the course.
  6. Document review: check identity and academic evidence, English, finances, genuine-student rationale, health/character and other destination-specific items.
  7. Application readiness: client confirms all information; authorised reviewer checks completeness and current rules before submission.
  8. After submission: track biometrics, medicals, requests, deadlines and decision. Explain visa conditions before travel.

Lesson 2 — Questions to ask the client

Study background

What is your highest qualification? When did you finish? Any study or employment gaps? What evidence can you provide?

English readiness

Have you taken IELTS/PTE or another accepted test? What score and date? Have you checked the institution's requirement?

Budget and funding

What is your available budget? Who funds you? Are funds accessible and supported by genuine evidence?

Study purpose

Why this course, provider and country? How does it connect to prior study or career plans?

Privacy: do not put passport numbers, bank account numbers, passwords or unnecessary sensitive personal data into this training page or an unsecured browser form.

Lesson 3 — Financial planning checklist

Tuition

First-year amount, deposits, instalment dates and refund rules.

Living

Accommodation, food, transport, utilities and personal expenses.

Application

Visa fee, biometrics, medical, insurance and document costs.

Travel & reserve

Flights, arrival setup and emergency reserve.

Immigration minimum-funds rules are not the same as a realistic full study budget. Check the exact current amount, currency, evidence format, holding period and exemptions for the applicant.

Lesson 4 — Source verification standard

  1. Start with the official government immigration authority, legislation or regulator.
  2. Confirm the rule applies to the correct visa category and applicant location.
  3. Record published/updated date separately from the effective date.
  4. Check transitional arrangements, exceptions and linked policy guidance.
  5. Save the exact source link and date checked; ask a senior reviewer when unclear.
  6. Never treat a website text change or automated monitor alert as proof that the law changed.

VisaWatch principle: Detection is not confirmation. A human reviewer must verify and approve legal interpretations before they are used for client advice.
